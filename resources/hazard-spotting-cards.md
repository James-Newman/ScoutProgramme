# Hazard Spotting Cards

Used in: [Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness](../year-1/term-2/outdoor-safety-and-risk-awareness.md) ("Safety induction: hazard cards, buddy check, STOP", 8 min). Six cards, one per patrol — each describes a bad-practice situation.

**Card 1**
"A Scout wanders off from the group to look at something interesting, without telling anyone."
*What's wrong: no one knows where they are; nobody's watching out for them.
What should happen: always tell a leader or buddy before going anywhere, even close by.*

**Card 2**
"A Scout goes exploring near the campsite at dusk with no torch."
*What's wrong: can't see hazards, can't be seen, will struggle to find the way back as it gets darker.
What should happen: take a torch any time it's getting dark, and don't go off alone.*

**Card 3**
"The group heads out for the day without checking the weather forecast, and nobody brought a coat."
*What's wrong: caught out by rain or cold with no way to stay warm/dry.
What should happen: check the forecast before heading out, dress for the forecast not the season.*

**Card 4**
"A Scout leaves their whistle and basic kit behind because 'it's just a short walk.'"
*What's wrong: no way to signal for help if something goes wrong, even on a short walk.
What should happen: basic safety kit (whistle, torch) comes along every time, regardless of how short the activity looks.*

**Card 5**
"Two Scouts split off from their buddy to go to the toilet block alone, in different directions."
*What's wrong: breaks the buddy system; if either has a problem, no one else knows.
What should happen: buddies stick together, or at minimum tell a leader where you're both going.*

**Card 6**
"A Scout notices a trip hazard (a guy line across a path) but doesn't say anything because it's 'not their problem.'"
*What's wrong: someone else will trip on it.
What should happen: flag hazards to a leader or fix them (if safe to do so) as soon as they're spotted.*
