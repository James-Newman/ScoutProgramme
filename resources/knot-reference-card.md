# Knot Reference Card

Used in: [Knots I](../year-2/term-2/knots-i.md) (one per patrol) and [Knots II](../year-2/term-2/knots-ii.md) ("Six-knot circuit", one per station). Covers all six knots Pioneer Activity Badge asks for. Print one per patrol or station, laminate if reusing across both sessions.

## Reef knot

Joins two ropes of equal thickness. "Left over right and under, then right over left and under." Both ends should end up on the same side, lying flat against each other — if it capsizes into a slip knot when pulled, one of the two steps was done the wrong way round.

## Clove hitch

Attaches a rope to a pole or post. Wrap the rope round the pole twice, crossing over itself the second time, then tuck the working end under the last crossing. Tightens under load but can slip along a smooth pole if not backed up.

## Round turn and two half hitches

Securely attaches a rope to a post, ring, or spar under load. Take a full turn round the anchor point, then tie two half hitches (a simple overhand loop repeated twice) around the standing part.

## Bowline

A fixed loop that won't slip or jam, even under heavy load. "The rabbit comes out of the hole, round the tree, and back down the hole" — the working end (rabbit) comes up through a small loop (hole), round the standing part (tree), and back down through the same loop.

## Sheet bend

Joins two ropes of different thickness, where a reef knot would fail. Form a loop in the thicker rope, pass the thinner rope up through the loop, round the back of it, and tuck it under itself (not under the loop).

## Timber hitch

Attaches a rope to a log or pole for dragging or hoisting. Pass the rope round the log, then twist the working end round the standing part several times — the more the load pulls, the tighter it grips.

## Notes

- Pair with a physical demonstration — a written reference on its own isn't enough for most Scouts to learn a new knot from scratch, but it's a good memory-jogger during practice and the six-knot circuit.
