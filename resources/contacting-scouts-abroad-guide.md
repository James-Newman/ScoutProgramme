# Contacting Scouts Abroad — Guide

Used in: [International Issues & Connections](../year-1/term-1/international-issues-and-connections.md) ("Reach out"). Read the Scout Association's own [International Links Guidance](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/running-your-section/programme-guidance/international-scouts-and-events/) before starting — it covers safeguarding and safety considerations for contacting people outside the UK, which take priority over anything below.

## Where to look for a contact

- **An existing link.** Ask your District/County International representative whether the Group already has a partnership with a Scout Group abroad — this is the easiest and safest starting point if one exists.
- **A national Scout association's social media.** Most national Scout associations (search "[country] Scouts" or check the [World Organization of the Scout Movement member list](https://www.scout.org/worldwide)) have a public Facebook or Instagram presence that can be messaged or commented on.
- **JOTA/JOTI.** Jamboree on the Air / Jamboree on the Internet is an annual event specifically for this kind of contact — worth flagging to leaders as a future opportunity even if it doesn't line up with this session's timing.

## Drafting the message

Keep it short and friendly. A good message includes:

1. Who you are — Troop name, town, country.
2. Why you're writing — "we're working on our World Challenge Award and wanted to find out about Scouting where you are."
3. One or two genuine questions — what does a normal meeting look like for them? What badges or activities are popular locally? Do they know of the international issue your patrol researched, and is it something that affects them?
4. A way to reply, and a thank you.

## After sending

A reply might take days, weeks, or might not come at all — that's normal, and doesn't affect whether the requirement is met. If a reply does come back (even after this term ends), it's worth sharing with the group.

## Notes

- All correspondence should go through a leader's supervised account, not a young person's personal social media or email, per standard safeguarding practice.
