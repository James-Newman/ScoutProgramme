# International Issue Briefing Cards

Used in: [International Issues & Connections](../year-1/term-1/international-issues-and-connections.md) ("Choosing an issue" / "Research the issue"). A fallback starting point for patrols without internet access, or patrols who want a jumping-off point before digging deeper themselves. Figures change over time — treat these as a starting point, not the final word, and encourage patrols to look up current numbers from the linked sources.

## Climate change

A global rise in average temperatures, driven mainly by greenhouse gas emissions, is changing weather patterns, sea levels, and ecosystems worldwide — with effects felt unevenly, often hardest in countries that contributed least to causing it. Start here: [UN climate action](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange).

## Access to clean water

Millions of people worldwide still lack access to safely managed drinking water, particularly in parts of sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, with knock-on effects for health, education (especially for girls, who often collect water), and daily life. Start here: [UN-Water](https://www.unwater.org/).

## Education inequality

Millions of children and young people worldwide are out of school or receiving a lower-quality education than their peers elsewhere, for reasons including poverty, conflict, gender, and disability. Start here: [UNESCO education data](https://www.unesco.org/en/education).

## Plastic pollution

Large volumes of plastic waste enter the world's oceans and rivers every year, harming marine life and, increasingly, entering the food chain as microplastics. Start here: [UN Environment Programme on plastic pollution](https://www.unep.org/plastic-pollution).

## Displacement and refugees

Tens of millions of people worldwide have been forced from their homes by conflict, persecution, or disaster, and are living as refugees or internally displaced people, often for years at a time. Start here: [UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/).

## Notes

- Encourage patrols to find one specific, current fact or figure from the linked source rather than relying solely on the summary above — that's where the "find out as much as you can" spirit of the requirement actually lives.
- These five are suggestions, not a fixed list — a patrol with a strong existing interest in a different international issue should be free to follow it instead.
