# Experiment 1: Run, Change & Retry

**Term:** Year 3, Term 2 — Scouting Science

**Fits the term because:** completes the first of the term's three required experiments, following on from [Being a Scientist: Plan Experiment 1](being-a-scientist-plan-experiment-1.md).

**Badge focus:** Scientist Activity Badge requirement — completing Experiment 1: changing one variable, trying again, checking the prediction, and understanding the science behind the result.

> "Change something about the experiment or activity and try it again, at least once. Predict what you think will happen and find out if you were right." "Show that you understand the science behind your experiment or activity."

## Session detail

### Welcome & recap (10 min)

Notices, opening. Recap each patrol's plan and prediction from last session.

### Run 1: fire-lighting speed (30 min)

Each patrol runs their fire-lighting comparison exactly as planned, timing how long each method/material takes to produce a sustained flame, under close leader supervision throughout.

**Materials:** fire-lighting kit per patrol's plan (tinder, matches/flint-and-steel, a metal tray, water/extinguisher on hand), stopwatches.

### Change one thing (20 min)

Each patrol changes the one variable from their plan (a different tinder, a drier/damper sample, a different lighting method) and re-predicts what they now expect to happen, given what Run 1 showed.

### Run 2 (30 min)

Each patrol repeats the experiment with their changed variable, timing it the same way as Run 1.

### The science behind it (20 min)

As a group, discuss the actual science behind fire-lighting: the fire triangle (heat, fuel, oxygen), why dry tinder catches faster (less energy wasted evaporating water first), and why some materials (very fine, high-surface-area tinder) catch quicker than others. Each patrol explains, in their own words, why their two runs came out the way they did.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs a fire risk assessment and appropriate supervision/extinguishing equipment, following the group's own safety policy for fire-lighting activities.
- Leaders should note each patrol's two timed results, their prediction versus what actually happened, and their explanation of the underlying science, as the evidence for this experiment.
