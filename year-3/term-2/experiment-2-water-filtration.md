# Experiment 2: Water Filtration

**Term:** Year 3, Term 2 — Scouting Science

**Fits the term because:** the second of the term's three required experiments — now that the plan/run/change/retry process is familiar from Experiment 1, this session runs the whole cycle in one sitting.

**Badge focus:** Scientist Activity Badge requirement — completing Experiment 2: planning, predicting, running, changing one variable, retrying, and understanding the science behind a simple water filter.

> "Plan and complete three science experiments or activities. Check your plan with an adult first, then for each experiment: 'Change something about the experiment or activity and try it again, at least once. Predict what you think will happen and find out if you were right.' 'Show that you understand the science behind your experiment or activity.'"

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain today's experiment: building a simple layered water filter (sand, gravel, charcoal, cloth) and testing how well it clears deliberately dirtied water.

### Plan & predict (20 min)

Each patrol decides what they'll change between their two filters — the order of layers, swapping charcoal for extra sand, adding a cloth pre-filter — and predicts which version will produce clearer water and why.

**Materials:** [experiment-plan template](../../resources/experiment-plan-template.md), one per patrol.

### Adult sign-off (5 min)

A quick safety check per patrol's plan (nothing hazardous in the "dirty" water — mud, tea, or food colouring only, never anything genuinely unsafe) before building begins.

### Build & run filter 1 (30 min)

Each patrol builds their first filter design in a cut plastic bottle (layers of gravel, sand, and charcoal, held with cloth or cotton wool), pours a measured amount of deliberately dirtied water through, and compares the result against the original — by eye, or with a simple clarity scale.

**Materials:** cut plastic bottles, gravel, sand, charcoal, cloth/cotton wool, a jug of water deliberately dirtied with mud/food colouring, clear collection cups.

### Change one thing (15 min)

Each patrol changes the one variable from their plan and re-predicts what they now expect, given filter 1's result.

### Build & run filter 2 (30 min)

Each patrol builds their changed filter design and repeats the test the same way as filter 1.

### Close: the science behind it (10 min)

Quick round: each patrol explains why their changed filter did better or worse — finer material traps more particles but can slow the flow, charcoal absorbs some impurities/odours, more layers generally mean cleaner (but slower) filtering. Note this doesn't produce safe drinking water — it's a demonstration of the physical filtering principle, not real purification.

## Notes

- Filtered water from this experiment is not safe to drink — make this explicit to the group.
- Leaders should note each patrol's two filter designs, their results, and their explanation of the underlying science, as the evidence for this experiment.
