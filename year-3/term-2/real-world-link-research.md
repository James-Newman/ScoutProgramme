# Real-World Link Research

**Term:** Year 3, Term 2 — Scouting Science

**Fits the term because:** with all three experiments now complete, this session researches the badge's final requirement properly before presenting it at [Science Showcase Night](science-showcase-night.md).

**Badge focus:** Scientist Activity Badge requirement — finding out how one experiment links to the real world.

> "Find out how one of your experiments or activities links to the real world. Then, explain it to others."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:30 | 20 min | Choose your experiment |
| 0:30–1:20 | 50 min | Research the real-world link |
| 1:20–1:50 | 30 min | Prepare your explanation |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain today's task: each patrol picks one of their three experiments (fire-lighting, water filtration, or insulation) and researches a genuine real-world application of it.

### Choose your experiment (20 min)

Each patrol picks whichever of their three experiments had the most interesting result, or the clearest real-world link to explore.

### Research the real-world link (50 min)

Depending on the experiment chosen, research one of:

- **Fire-lighting** — how wildfire spreads (fuel, heat, oxygen at a landscape scale), or how historical/survival fire-starting methods connect to today's understanding of combustion.
- **Water filtration** — how real water treatment works (municipal treatment plants, or portable filters used in humanitarian aid and disaster relief, connecting back to [Natural Disaster Investigation](../../year-2/term-3/natural-disaster-investigation.md) from Year 2, Term 3, if any Scouts remember it).
- **Insulation** — how insulation is used in real outdoor equipment (sleeping bag ratings, thermal jackets) or in buildings (loft/cavity wall insulation, energy efficiency).

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or pre-printed briefing notes as a fallback.

### Prepare your explanation (30 min)

Each patrol prepares a short explanation (a poster, a few index cards, or just well-organised talking points) connecting their experiment's result to the real-world application they researched, ready to present at [Science Showcase Night](science-showcase-night.md).

**Materials:** poster paper/card, pens.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Push patrols towards a genuine, specific connection ("water filtration is used in disaster relief because clean water access often fails first") rather than a vague, generic link.
- Leaders should note each patrol's chosen experiment and real-world link, ready to check the explanation itself at Science Showcase Night.
