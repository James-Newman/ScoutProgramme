# Science Behind Scouting / Illustrate a Scene from a Story

**Term:** Year 3, Term 2 — Science & Art

**Fits the term because:** the first parallel-track session following [Science & Art Taster](science-and-art-taster.md) — Scouts join whichever half matches their chosen track.

**Badge focus:** Science track — Scientist Activity Badge requirement, exploring the science behind two Scouting activities or hobbies. Art track — Artist Activity Badge requirement, the first of three required illustrations.

> **Scientist:** "Explore and discuss the science behind two Scouting activities or hobbies."

> **Artist:** "Paint, draw or illustrate each of these: a scene from a story, a person or object, a landscape."

## Running order (both tracks run in parallel)

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome (joint) |
| 0:10–1:40 | 90 min | Track content |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Track share-back |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close (joint) |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min, joint)

Notices, opening, split into Science and Art groups.

### Science track: The Science Behind Scouting (90 min)

In small groups, Scouts pick two Scouting activities or hobbies (fire-lighting, knots and pulleys, orienteering/compasses, cooking, camping in cold weather) and dig into the actual science behind each — why fires need the fire triangle, the physics of knots and friction, how a magnetic compass works, the chemistry of cooking, how insulation keeps a tent warm. Each group produces a simple explanation (a poster, a diagram, or notes) they can talk through confidently.

**Materials:** research access (internet or pre-printed science-behind-Scouting briefing notes), poster paper/pens for write-ups.

### Art track: Illustrate a Scene from a Story (90 min)

Each Scout chooses a favourite book, film, or myth and illustrates a scene from it — painting, drawing, or another illustration medium, whichever they're most comfortable with. Encourage a genuine attempt at composition (more than one element in the scene) rather than a single isolated object.

**Materials:** paper, pencils, paints/coloured pencils/markers, reference material if a Scout wants to look up their chosen scene.

### Track share-back (10 min, within each track)

Science groups briefly explain one of their two activities' science to the room; Art Scouts hold up their scene illustration and name what story it's from.

### Close (10 min, joint)

Both tracks reconvene, notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Science and Art content run in the same time slot in separate spaces — needs a leader (or confident helper) per track.
- Leaders should note each Science group's two chosen activities and each Art Scout's finished scene illustration, as evidence for their respective requirements.
