# Experiment 3: Insulation Test

**Term:** Year 3, Term 2 — Scouting Science

**Fits the term because:** the third and final of the term's three required experiments, run in the same single-sitting shape as [Experiment 2](experiment-2-water-filtration.md) now that the process is well established.

**Badge focus:** Scientist Activity Badge requirement — completing Experiment 3: planning, predicting, running, changing one variable, retrying, and understanding the science behind insulation.

> "Plan and complete three science experiments or activities. Check your plan with an adult first, then for each experiment: 'Change something about the experiment or activity and try it again, at least once. Predict what you think will happen and find out if you were right.' 'Show that you understand the science behind your experiment or activity.'"

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain today's experiment: which material keeps a cup of warm water warmest for longest — relevant to choosing camping gear (sleeping bags, flasks, insulated jackets).

### Plan & predict (20 min)

Each patrol picks a material to wrap their test cup in (wool, bubble wrap, foil, cotton wool, newspaper) and predicts how it'll perform against a plain, unwrapped control cup, and why.

**Materials:** [experiment-plan template](../../resources/experiment-plan-template.md), one per patrol.

### Adult sign-off (5 min)

A quick safety check — warm (not scalding) water only, thermometers handled carefully.

### Wrap & measure, test 1 (30 min)

Each patrol wraps their cup in their chosen material, pours in the same measured amount of warm water as every other patrol (poured from a shared kettle/urn at the same time for a fair comparison), and takes a temperature reading every five minutes for twenty minutes.

**Materials:** identical cups, insulating materials (wool, bubble wrap, foil, cotton wool, newspaper), thermometers, a kettle or urn of warm water, timers.

### Change one thing (15 min)

Each patrol changes the one variable from their plan (a different material, a thicker layer, adding a lid) and re-predicts what they now expect.

### Wrap & measure, test 2 (30 min)

Each patrol repeats the test with their changed variable, following the same measuring routine as test 1.

### Close: the science behind it (10 min)

Quick round: each patrol explains why their changed version performed better or worse — trapped air is a poor conductor of heat, which is why fluffy materials (wool, bubble wrap) usually insulate better than dense ones, and why more layers (more trapped air) generally do better still.

## Notes

- Warm water only, not boiling — check with a thermometer before pouring rather than guessing.
- Leaders should note each patrol's two test results (temperature readings over time), their prediction versus outcome, and their explanation of the underlying science, as the evidence for this experiment — this completes all three required experiments, ready for [Real-World Link Research](real-world-link-research.md).
