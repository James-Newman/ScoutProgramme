# Conservation Project: Wrap-Up & Litter-Pick

**Term:** Year 3, Term 3 — Environmental Conservation

**Fits the term because:** the third and final piece of this term's conservation project — a genuine, local, hands-on action close to home, plus a chance to reflect on the whole project before the term closes.

**Badge focus:** Environmental Conservation Activity Badge requirement — completes the activity/project requirement (part 3 of 3, alongside [Site Visit & Habitat Task](conservation-project-site-visit-and-habitat-task.md) and [Recycling & Energy Audit](recycling-and-energy-audit.md)).

> "Take part in an activity or project that improves local conservation." Areas this can cover include recycling, energy reduction, habitat protection, pollution, erosion, or flooding.

## Running order

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain today's task and that it's the final piece of the term's conservation project, following [Site Visit & Habitat Task](conservation-project-site-visit-and-habitat-task.md) and [Recycling & Energy Audit](recycling-and-energy-audit.md).

### Safety brief (15 min)

Cover litter-picking safety: gloves at all times, litter-pickers rather than bare hands for anything sharp, never touch anything that looks hazardous (needles, broken glass) — flag it to a leader instead, high-vis if working near roads.

**Materials:** litter-pickers, gloves, high-vis vests, refuse sacks.

### Local litter-pick (70 min)

Patrols litter-pick a genuinely local area — a park, a stretch of riverside, streets near the meeting place. Split the area into patrol-sized sections so effort doesn't overlap, and weigh or count filled bags at the end for a satisfying, concrete measure of the impact.

**Materials:** litter-pickers, gloves, refuse sacks, a local area map showing which patrol covers which section.

### Project reflection (20 min)

As a group, reflect on the whole conservation project across the term: the site visit and habitat task, the recycling and energy audit, and today's litter-pick. What real difference did the Troop make? What's the single change from the recycling audit still ongoing? This reflection is good evidence that the project was genuinely understood, not just completed as separate disconnected tasks.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Coordinate with the local council or landowner if litter-picking somewhere other than genuinely public land, and arrange collected rubbish disposal in advance.
- Leaders should note the litter-pick's concrete outcome (bags collected, area covered) and engagement with the reflection, as the final piece of evidence for this requirement, alongside the previous two project sessions.
