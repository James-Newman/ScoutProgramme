# Campaign: Choose Your Target & Plan

**Term:** Year 3, Term 3 — Environmental Conservation

**Fits the term because:** starts the badge's awareness-campaign requirement in earnest, building on the term's focus issue from [Environmental Issue Research](environmental-issue-research.md).

**Badge focus:** Environmental Conservation Activity Badge requirement — getting involved in a campaign to raise awareness of an environmental issue (planning half).

> "Get involved in a campaign to make others aware of an environmental issue." Examples include writing to MPs and local agencies or engaging with community groups.

| Time | Duration | Block |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Recap the term's focus environmental issue from [Environmental Issue Research](environmental-issue-research.md).

### Who can actually make a difference? (20 min)

As a group, brainstorm who could realistically act on the term's focus issue — the local MP, the district or borough council, a relevant local agency (Environment Agency, a wildlife trust), or a community group/local business. Discuss what each might actually be able to do.

### Choose your target (30 min)

In patrols, decide who the campaign should genuinely target — an MP, a local agency, or a community group — and what specific action or change they're asking for. Push for something concrete and realistic rather than a vague "raise awareness" goal with no actual ask.

### Plan the campaign (40 min)

Each patrol plans their approach — a letter or email campaign to an MP/agency, an information stall or poster campaign engaging a community group, or a mix. Draft the core message now, ready for [Campaign: Take Action](campaign-take-action.md) next session.

**Materials:** paper and pens for drafting; internet access to look up the correct contact/address for the chosen target.

### Share plans (15 min)

Each patrol shares their target and planned approach with the room, so everyone's clear before next session's action.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Finding the correct contact details (an MP's constituency office, a specific agency team, a community group's actual contact) takes real research — leave enough time for it, or prepare a fallback contact list in advance.
- Leaders should note each patrol's chosen target and planned approach, ready to check the actual action taken next session.
