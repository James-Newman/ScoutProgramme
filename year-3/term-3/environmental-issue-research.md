# Environmental Issue Research

**Term:** Year 3, Term 3 — Environmental Conservation

**Fits the term because:** the term's opening piece of badge content — researching a genuine local environmental issue sets up the practical project and awareness campaign that follow.

**Badge focus:** Environmental Conservation Activity Badge requirement — finding out about an environmental issue important to the local community.

> "Find out about an environmental issue that is important to your local community." Potential topics include recycling and conservation, energy efficiency, local land use, pollution, coastal erosion, or tourism impact.

| Time | Duration | Block |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain the term's shape: today's research sets up [Conservation Project: Site Visit & Habitat Task](conservation-project-site-visit-and-habitat-task.md) and the awareness campaign later in the term.

### What matters here? (20 min)

As a group, brainstorm environmental issues that might affect the local area — the badge's own examples (recycling, energy efficiency, local land use, pollution, coastal erosion, tourism impact) are a good prompt list, but push for genuinely local specifics rather than generic global issues.

### Research in patrols (40 min)

Each patrol picks one local environmental issue from the brainstorm and researches it properly — local news coverage, council publications, or a local wildlife trust/conservation group's own materials if available. Push for real local detail (a specific river, a specific green space, a specific council scheme) over vague generalities.

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or pre-printed local news/council briefing packs as a fallback.

### Present back (30 min)

Each patrol presents their researched issue to the room — what it is, why it matters locally, and who's affected.

### Choose the term's focus issue (15 min)

As a group, choose (or combine) one issue from today's research to carry forward as the term's practical focus — this becomes the target for the awareness campaign later in the term, and should ideally connect to whatever local conservation project gets arranged for [Conservation Project: Site Visit & Habitat Task](conservation-project-site-visit-and-habitat-task.md).

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Try to pick a term-focus issue that a real local conservation partner (nature reserve, wildlife trust, council environmental team) is already actively working on — it makes booking the practical placement and finding a genuine campaign target much easier.
- Leaders should note each patrol's researched issue and the group's chosen term-focus issue, as the evidence for this requirement.
