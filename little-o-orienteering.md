# Little O (Orienteering in the Park)

**Term:** every Autumn term (Term 1 of each Year) — copy this file into that term's directory and update this line.

**Fits the term because:** it's a standing requirement for every Autumn term, regardless of that term's own focus badge — a short, local orienteering session run in the park or woods, separate from the term's badge work.

**Badge focus:** Orienteer Activity Badge — complete in full this session.

> "Learn about the sport of Orienteering." You could find out about the different types of Orienteering, the equipment that is used and how to successfully complete a course.

> "Learn about the map colours and common symbols used on an orienteering map."

> "Orientate a map and use basic map skills." Learn how to navigate while keeping the map set to the ground. 'Thumb' the map to log your changing position and use the terrain to orientate yourself to your surroundings.

> "Complete three different Orienteering courses, these could be local courses or at Orienteering events."

Source: [Scouts Orienteer Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/orienteer/) — check the live page before formally signing anything off, since requirement wording is occasionally updated by the Scout Association.

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with an outdoor session in the local park or woods, longer than the standard slot to fit three courses in. Treat the running order below as a shape to adjust to the specific venue, not a fixed script.

## Running order (example, ~2 hours 15 minutes)

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Muster & kit check |
| 0:10–0:30 | 20 min | Learning orienteering & map symbols |
| 0:30–0:50 | 20 min | Setting the map & thumbing |
| 0:50–1:10 | 20 min | Course 1 (short, easy) |
| 1:10–1:30 | 20 min | Course 2 (medium) |
| 1:30–2:00 | 30 min | Course 3 (longer, harder) |
| 2:00–2:15 | 15 min | Debrief & collection |

## Session detail

### Muster & kit check (10 min)

Meet at the park/woods, register patrols, and check appropriate outdoor clothing and footwear for the terrain and forecast. Confirm buddy pairs and whistles, same convention as [Night Hike](year-1/term-1/night-hike.md).

### Learning orienteering & map symbols (20 min)

Cover the sport itself: the different types of orienteering (point-to-point score events, line courses), the equipment used (compass, control markers/punches or electronic dibbers, whistle), and roughly how a course works. Then teach the standard orienteering map colours and symbols (e.g. yellow for open land, green for vegetation density, blue for water, black for man-made features/rock, brown for contours/landform, purple for control circles and course lines) — a simplified printed symbol key works well as a takeaway reference for the courses that follow.

**Materials:** a simplified orienteering map-symbol key handout, one per Scout or pair.

### Setting the map & thumbing (20 min)

Teach orientating ("setting") the map to the ground using a compass or terrain features, and "thumbing" the map — using your thumb to mark your current position and updating it as you move. Practise with a short walk around the immediate area, checking each Scout can keep the map set as they turn and change direction.

**Materials:** printed orienteering maps of the park/woods, one per Scout or pair; compasses.

### Course 1 (20 min) — short, easy

The first of the three required courses: a short course with a handful of controls in easy, open terrain.

### Course 2 (20 min) — medium

A moderately harder course than Course 1 — more controls, or trickier terrain/navigation.

### Course 3 (30 min) — longer, harder

The third and most challenging course — longer, more controls, tougher terrain (into the woods rather than open park if the venue allows). Completing all three satisfies the badge's "three different courses" requirement in one outing.

**Materials:** three pre-set courses' worth of control markers/punches or electronic dibbers, control cards.

### Debrief & collection (15 min)

Head-count, a quick round on what went well and what was tricky across the three courses, and a reminder that this completes the Orienteer Activity Badge.

## Notes

- Needs three pre-set courses of increasing difficulty in the local park/woods, plus a risk assessment for the specific venue (other park users, terrain hazards, boundaries, weather).
- If the local park already has a permanent orienteering course installed (many do, via British Orienteering or the local council), that satisfies "local courses" too and cuts leader prep — worth checking before setting fresh controls from scratch each year.
- Leaders should note which Scouts completed all three courses as the evidence for this badge — this repo doesn't track individual Scout progress.
- Every Autumn term needs one of these — when a new Autumn term gets built out, copy this file into that term's directory, update the header, and reuse the same three-course structure (fresh courses if the venue or group has changed, the same ones if not).
