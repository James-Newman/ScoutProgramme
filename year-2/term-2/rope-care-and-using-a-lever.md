# Rope Care & Using a Lever

**Term:** Year 2, Term 2 — Knots & Pioneering

**Fits the term because:** covers the remaining Pioneer Activity Badge skills beyond knots and lashings — looking after rope properly and using a lever — ahead of putting it all together in [Indoor Pioneering Project](indoor-pioneering-project.md).

**Badge focus:** Pioneer Activity Badge requirement — whip or seal a rope end, splice a rope, coil and store rope correctly, use a lever, and explain pioneering safety.

> "Show how to do the following: whip or seal the end of a rope; make a suitable splice in a rope; correctly coil and store a rope; use a lever; explain how to keep yourself and others safe while taking part in pioneering activities."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Whipping and sealing rope ends (25 min)

Teach common whipping (binding a rope end with cord to stop it fraying) on natural-fibre rope, and heat-sealing (melting the end) on synthetic rope — a leader demonstrates the heat-sealing step under close supervision, Scouts don't handle the flame themselves for this part.

**Materials:** natural-fibre rope offcuts, whipping twine; synthetic rope offcuts, a lighter or heat source (leader-controlled).

### Back splice (30 min)

Teach a simple back splice (finishing a rope end by weaving the strands back into themselves — a sturdier, more permanent alternative to whipping). Fiddly and slow to learn, so keep expectations realistic — a rough-but-correct first attempt is a win.

**Materials:** thicker three-strand rope, one length per Scout or pair.

### Coiling and storing rope (20 min)

Teach a proper coil (e.g. the standard mountaineer's coil) that won't kink or tangle in storage, plus how to hang or store rope so it lasts (out of damp, out of direct sun, not left knotted). Race patrols to coil a rope correctly and quickly.

**Materials:** longer lengths of rope, one per patrol.

### Using a lever (20 min)

Teach using a simple lever (a pole and a fixed point, e.g. a log as a fulcrum) to move or lift a heavy object safely — demonstrate the mechanical advantage, then let each patrol have a go moving a genuinely heavy object (a full water container, a log) using a lever rather than brute force.

**Materials:** a sturdy pole per patrol, a fulcrum object (log, brick), something heavy to move.

### Pioneering safety (10 min)

A short, direct talk: spar and rope inspection before use, safe carrying of spars (ends down, mind other people), safe distances when raising a structure, and what to do if something starts to give way. This is the explaining-safety half of the requirement — keep it practical rather than a lecture, referencing the actual kit just used today.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Heat-sealing rope ends needs close leader supervision — Scouts shouldn't handle the flame themselves.
- Leaders should note which Scouts can demonstrate whipping/sealing, splicing, coiling, and using a lever, plus engagement with the safety talk, as the evidence for this requirement.
