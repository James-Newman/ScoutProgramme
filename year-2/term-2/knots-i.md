# Knots I

**Term:** Year 2, Term 2 — Knots & Pioneering

**Fits the term because:** the first of two dedicated knot-tying sessions working through the six knots Pioneer Activity Badge asks for.

**Badge focus:** Pioneer Activity Badge requirement — name and tie six or more different knots (first three of six).

> "Name and tie six or more different knots, and name and tie three or more different lashings."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:35 | 25 min | Reef knot |
| 0:35–1:00 | 25 min | Clove hitch |
| 1:00–1:40 | 40 min | Round turn and two half hitches |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Knot relay |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain that this term's knots and lashings are building blocks for the pioneering projects later in the term.

### Reef knot (25 min)

Teach the reef knot (joining two ropes of equal thickness) using the "left over right, right over left" rhyme. Practice in pairs until each Scout can tie it without prompting, then practice untying it too — a poorly tied reef knot capsizes, which is worth demonstrating.

**Materials:** one length of rope per Scout (roughly 1m).

### Clove hitch (25 min)

Teach the clove hitch (attaching a rope to a pole or post) — useful for starting and finishing most lashings later in the term, so it's worth over-teaching here. Practice tying it around a table leg or pioneering pole.

**Materials:** ropes from above; poles or table legs to tie around.

### Round turn and two half hitches (40 min)

Teach the round turn and two half hitches (securely attaching a rope to a post, ring, or spar, taking the load safely) — a slower teach since it has more steps than the previous two. Practice tying and re-tying until confident, then run a simple test: each Scout ties all three of today's knots from memory while a partner checks them against a reference card.

**Materials:** ropes and poles from above; a [knot reference card](../../resources/knot-reference-card.md) per patrol.

### Knot relay (10 min)

Patrol relay — one Scout at a time runs up, ties a named knot (called out by a leader), a leader checks it, then the next Scout goes. Fun, competitive, and a quick check on retention.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs enough short lengths of rope for one per Scout, plus poles/spars or sturdy table legs to tie around.
- Leaders should note which of today's three knots each Scout can tie unprompted, as the evidence for this requirement, ahead of [Knots II](knots-ii.md) covering the remaining three.
