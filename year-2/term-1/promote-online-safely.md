# Promote Online, Safely

**Term:** Year 2, Term 1 — Creative Challenge

**Fits the term because:** combines two Creative Challenge Award requirements that naturally belong together — using creative ability to promote something, and doing it safely online, since a promotional post is exactly the kind of creative internet use the badge has in mind.

**Badge focus:** Creative Challenge Award requirements — using creative ability to promote a Scouting activity or event, and using social media/the internet creatively and safely.

> "Use your creative ability to help promote a Scouting activity or event."

> "Use social media or the internet to be creative, and know how to stay safe when doing this."

| Time | Duration | Block |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Staying safe online (20 min)

Cover what "safe" actually means for this task: never sharing full names, addresses, schools, or exact locations of young people in anything public-facing; getting leader/parent sign-off before anything is actually posted; using the Group's official channels rather than personal accounts; being mindful of image consent. This isn't a caveat to rush through — it's an explicit part of the requirement.

### Pick the event (30 min)

In patrols, choose a real upcoming Scouting activity or event to promote — this term's Camp, an open evening, a district event, or a recruitment drive. Discuss who the audience is and what would actually make them want to come.

### Create the promotion (40 min)

Each patrol creates a piece of promotional content: a poster, a short video, a mock social media post (designed but not necessarily actually posted, unless a leader signs off doing so for real), or similar — using whatever creative skill they developed last session if it fits (a drawn poster, a short filmed drama sketch, a piece of music for a video).

**Materials:** paper/art materials for posters, or phones/tablets for video/digital work.

### Share & feedback (15 min)

Each patrol presents their promotional piece to the room. Quick feedback round: would this actually make you want to come?

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- If a patrol's piece is genuinely good enough to use for real, check with a leader about actually posting it on the Group's official channels — turning this into something with a real audience is a nice bonus, not a requirement.
- Leaders should note each patrol's promotional piece and engagement with the online-safety discussion as the evidence for these two requirements.
