# Natural Disaster Investigation

**Term:** Year 2, Term 3 — Global Issues

**Fits the term because:** the badge's own wording asks Scouts to move from research straight into practical thinking about how the Troop could actually help — this session does the research and decision-making half; [Troop Action Day](troop-action-day.md) carries it through.

**Badge focus:** Global Issues Activity Badge requirement — investigating a recent natural disaster and deciding how the Troop can assist.

> "Investigate a recent natural disaster by looking at photos, news articles or videos online" and decide in small groups what five things were needed to help, then determine how your Troop can assist.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Choosing a disaster |
| 0:20–1:00 | 40 min | Investigate |
| 1:00–1:30 | 30 min | Five things needed |
| 1:30–1:50 | 20 min | How can the Troop help? |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain the session's shape: research, decide what was needed, then decide what the Troop can actually do about it.

### Choosing a disaster (10 min)

Each patrol picks one recent natural disaster to investigate — an earthquake, flood, hurricane, or wildfire from the last year or two works well. Steer patrols towards genuine variety rather than every patrol picking the same headline event.

### Investigate (40 min)

Patrols look at photos, news articles, and videos online about their chosen disaster — what happened, where, and its scale and impact.

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or the [global issues briefing cards](../../resources/global-issues-briefing-cards.md) as a fallback for patrols without device access.

### Five things needed (30 min)

Each patrol decides on five things that were most needed to help those affected — could be physical (clean water, shelter, medical supplies), or less tangible (emergency funding, volunteers, communication infrastructure). Push for specifics tied to what they actually found in their research, not a generic list.

### How can the Troop help? (20 min)

Each patrol proposes one realistic way the Troop itself could help, given its size and resources — a fundraiser, a collection drive, an awareness campaign, or writing to a relevant charity. Patrols present their proposal to the room; as a group, pick (or combine) proposals to carry forward into [Troop Action Day](troop-action-day.md).

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home. Announce which proposal(s) got picked, so patrols can start thinking ahead to Troop Action Day.

## Notes

- This term makes a deliberate choice to carry the "how can the Troop help" question through into a genuine follow-up session ([Troop Action Day](troop-action-day.md)) rather than leaving it as a discussion exercise — the badge's own wording only asks Scouts to "determine" how the Troop can assist, so the follow-through is enrichment, not itself required.
- Leaders should note each patrol's five identified needs and their help proposal, as the evidence for this requirement.
