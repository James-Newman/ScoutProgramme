# International Sport & Peace

**Term:** Year 2, Term 3 — Global Issues

**Fits the term because:** a lighter, more active piece of badge content partway through the term, after two research-heavy sessions.

**Badge focus:** Global Issues Activity Badge requirement — finding out about an international sporting event and discussing how sport contributes to international peace and understanding.

> "Find out about an international sporting event and discuss with your Troop how sporting events can contribute to international peace and understanding."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:40 | 30 min | Research an event |
| 0:40–1:00 | 20 min | Opening ceremony moments |
| 1:00–1:40 | 40 min | Mini international games |
| 1:40–1:55 | 15 min | Discussion: sport and peace |
| 1:55–2:00 | 5 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Research an event (30 min)

In patrols, research one international sporting event — the Olympics, the FIFA World Cup, the Commonwealth Games, or the Paralympics all work well. Find out how many countries take part, where it's next being held, and one notable moment of nations coming together despite political tension (a famous example: North and South Korea marching together at the 2018 Winter Olympics opening ceremony).

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or the [global issues briefing cards](../../resources/global-issues-briefing-cards.md) as a fallback.

### Opening ceremony moments (20 min)

As a group, look at a few famous opening-ceremony moments (photos or short video clips) where sport visibly brought nations together — flag parades, joint teams, athletes from opposing countries competing side by side shortly after conflict.

**Materials:** a way to show photos/video clips to the room (projector, tablet, printed photos).

### Mini international games (40 min)

Run a light-hearted "mini Olympics" — patrols represent different countries (assign or let them pick) and compete in three or four simple games (a relay, a target game, a tug of war). Purely for fun and atmosphere, not itself a requirement — it just makes the theme tangible before the discussion.

**Materials:** whatever the chosen games need — cones, a rope for tug of war, bean bags for a target game.

### Discussion: sport and peace (15 min)

As a group, discuss how sporting events can contribute to international peace and understanding — using today's research and the opening-ceremony moments as concrete examples rather than an abstract discussion.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- The mini international games are optional colour, not required — cut them if time's short and the discussion needs more room.
- Leaders should note each patrol's researched event and engagement with the closing discussion, as the evidence for this requirement.
