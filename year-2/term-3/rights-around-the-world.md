# Rights Around the World

**Term:** Year 2, Term 3 — Global Issues

**Fits the term because:** the last of the term's five Global Issues Activity Badge requirements, and a natural closing piece of badge content ahead of Camp — a reflective session rather than an active one.

**Badge focus:** Global Issues Activity Badge requirement — comparing UK rights with the rights a Scout in another country might have.

> "Think about the rights that we have in the UK (like the right to vote, the right to go to school and equal gender rights)" and compare these with rights a Scout in another country might have.

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Our rights in the UK (20 min)

As a group, list out the rights Scouts in the UK have — the right to vote (at the appropriate age), the right to go to school, equal gender rights, freedom of speech, freedom of religion. Keep it concrete rather than abstract; the badge's own examples are a good starting list.

### Choose a country and compare (40 min)

In patrols, choose a different country and research what rights are like there for someone the same age as a Scout — access to education, voting age, gender equality, freedom of expression. Push for genuine variety of countries across patrols, and for specifics over vague impressions.

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or the [global issues briefing cards](../../resources/global-issues-briefing-cards.md) as a fallback.

### Scout-to-Scout comparison (30 min)

Each patrol imagines a Scout their own age living in their researched country and writes a short "day in the life" comparison against a UK Scout's day — what's different about school, free time, and the rights they can rely on.

### Group discussion (15 min)

Bring the patrols' findings together as a whole-group discussion: what surprised people, and what rights do UK Scouts perhaps take for granted.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- This session and [International Sport & Peace](international-sport-and-peace.md) both touch "other countries," but from genuinely distinct angles (rights and daily life, versus sport and diplomacy) — no requirement overlap between them.
- Leaders should note each patrol's researched country and the comparison produced, as the evidence for this requirement.
