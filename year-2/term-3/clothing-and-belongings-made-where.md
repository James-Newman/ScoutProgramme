# Clothing & Belongings: Made Where?

**Term:** Year 2, Term 3 — Global Issues

**Fits the term because:** the term's opening piece of badge content — a hands-on, concrete way into "global issues" that starts with something every Scout is already wearing.

**Badge focus:** Global Issues Activity Badge requirement — identifying where clothing/belongings are made and discussing overseas production.

> "Identify where your clothing or other belongings have been made" and discuss with other Scouts why companies choose overseas production and its advantages/disadvantages.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:30 | 20 min | Label hunt |
| 0:30–0:50 | 20 min | Map it |
| 0:50–1:30 | 40 min | Why overseas? |

| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Label hunt (20 min)

Each Scout checks the "made in" label on a few items they're wearing or carrying (coat, trainers, phone case, watch, bag) and notes the country for each.

### Map it (20 min)

As a group, mark every country found on a large wall map or floor map with a pin or sticky note. Talk about the pattern that emerges — most items cluster in a handful of countries/regions.

**Materials:** a large world map (wall or floor), pins or sticky notes.

### Why overseas? (40 min)

In patrols, research why companies choose to manufacture overseas rather than in the UK — labour costs, specialist manufacturing hubs, established supply chains. Each patrol picks one real example item (trainers, phones, t-shirts) and traces its likely journey from raw material to shop shelf.

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets), or the [global issues briefing cards](../../resources/global-issues-briefing-cards.md) as a fallback.

### Advantages and disadvantages debate (20 min)

Split the room into two sides for a light, structured debate: one side argues the advantages of overseas production (cost, jobs created abroad, specialist expertise), the other the disadvantages (environmental cost of shipping, working conditions, loss of local manufacturing jobs). Swap sides partway through so everyone argues both.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- A world map that can stay up for the rest of the term is useful — several later sessions (International Sport & Peace, Rights Around the World) also reference countries.
- Leaders should note each Scout's identified items/countries and engagement with the discussion and debate, as the evidence for this requirement.
