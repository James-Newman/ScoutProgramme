# Year 2, Term 3 (Summer) — Global Issues

Term summary: [`data/terms.yaml`](../../data/terms.yaml) (year 2, term 3) · [`docs/timeline.md`](../../docs/timeline.md)

Core goals: tracing where clothing and belongings are made, researching a recent natural disaster and how the Troop could help, exploring how an international sporting event contributes to peace and understanding, sharing an international health issue creatively, and comparing rights in the UK with those of a Scout in another country.
Badges earned: **Global Issues Activity Badge**.
Optional staged progression: Nights Away (+2), Time on the Water.

This term resolves two flags left open at the top of `data/terms.yaml`. First, a naming error: the catalogue previously listed a "Global Issues Challenge Award," which isn't a real Scout Association badge — there are only 9 real Challenge Awards for the Scouts section. It was most likely always meant to be the real **Global Issues Activity Badge**, now correctly named here. Second, this resolves the displacement of that content from Year 1, Term 2, when that term became the Emergency Aid term. It's also this term's replacement badge after Paddle Sports Staged Activity Badge (Stage 1), the previous Year 2, Term 3 focus, moved to passive — see `filler-sessions/paddle-sports-staged-activity-badge.md`.

Every term's 12 weeks follow the same shape: one [Welcome Session](welcome-session.md), one [Night Hike](night-hike.md), one [Camp](camp.md), one [External Activity](external-activity.md), and one [End of Term Fun & Games](end-of-term-fun-and-games.md) session — five fixed slots every term, regardless of theme. This is a Summer term, so there's no Shooting Session or Little O this time round — those only recur in each year's Autumn term. That leaves seven weeks for the term's own badge content.

**This term needs only 5 of those 7 core-content weeks for Global Issues Activity Badge itself.** [Clothing & Belongings: Made Where?](clothing-and-belongings-made-where.md), [Natural Disaster Investigation](natural-disaster-investigation.md), [International Sport & Peace](international-sport-and-peace.md), [International Health Issue: Creative Share](international-health-issue-creative-share.md), and [Rights Around the World](rights-around-the-world.md) cover the badge's five requirements one session each. **That leaves 2 of the term's 7 core-content weeks free.** One goes to [Troop Action Day](troop-action-day.md) — a deliberate enrichment session that follows through on Natural Disaster Investigation's "determine how your Troop can assist" wording with a genuine (if modest) action, rather than leaving it as a discussion exercise. The other carries [Media Relations & Marketing](../../filler-sessions/media-relations-and-marketing.md) — a complete, standalone badge in its own right, chosen because its "get a message to an audience" shape pairs naturally with this term's global-issues and Troop Action Day content, and because it's still unused after seven terms.

**Passive-completion check:** this term's international content (sport, rights, natural disasters) is distinct in shape from the World Challenge Award's international-issue requirement covered back in Year 1, Term 1 ([International Issues & Connections](../../year-1/term-1/international-issues-and-connections.md), which used climate change/water/education-style issues and Scout-to-Scout contact) — no requirement double-counts between them.

## Requirement coverage

All 5 Global Issues Activity Badge requirements are covered this term:

> "Identify where your clothing or other belongings have been made" and discuss with other Scouts why companies choose overseas production and its advantages/disadvantages. — [Clothing & Belongings: Made Where?](clothing-and-belongings-made-where.md)
>
> "Investigate a recent natural disaster by looking at photos, news articles or videos online" and decide in small groups what five things were needed to help, then determine how your Troop can assist. — [Natural Disaster Investigation](natural-disaster-investigation.md)
>
> "Find out about an international sporting event and discuss with your Troop how sporting events can contribute to international peace and understanding." — [International Sport & Peace](international-sport-and-peace.md)
>
> "In small groups, learn about an international health issue and use a creative way to share what you have learnt with your Troop" (examples include Malaria, Ebola, HIV and Aids). — [International Health Issue: Creative Share](international-health-issue-creative-share.md)
>
> "Think about the rights that we have in the UK (like the right to vote, the right to go to school and equal gender rights)" and compare these with rights a Scout in another country might have. — [Rights Around the World](rights-around-the-world.md)

Source: [Scouts Global Issues Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/global-issues/) — check the live page before formally signing anything off, since requirement wording is occasionally updated by the Scout Association.
