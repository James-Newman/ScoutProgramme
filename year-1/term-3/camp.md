# Camp

**Term:** Year 1, Term 3 — Fire & Cooking

**Fits the term because:** every term includes one Camp regardless of theme — but this term, Camp is where the real substance of the Chef Activity Badge happens. Patrols cook and serve the two-course menu they planned in [Menu Planning, Shopping & Storage](menu-planning-shopping-and-storage.md), using the fire and knife skills taught in [Fire Safety & Camp Cooking Prep](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/fire-safety-and-camp-cooking.md) and the site they laid out in [Site Layout & Design](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/site-layout-design.md). The badge's own wording explicitly allows the meal to be "prepared on an open fire on camp," which is exactly what happens here.

**Badge focus:** Chef Activity Badge requirements — cooking and serving a two-course meal, and clearing up safely afterwards. Also the standing home for three Outdoor Challenge Award requirements this term (Nights Away, explore/respect the environment, and the well-organised site this term's patrols have specifically planned for).

> "Cook and serve at least one meal from your menu, including at least two courses. This can be prepared on an open fire on camp, on a tranjia or stove, or in a kitchen. Think about how you can avoid food waste when preparing and serving your meal."

> "Demonstrate how to clear up and manage leftovers safely and responsibly. This should include washing up utensils, pots and pans and the cooking area; reusing, composting or recycling where possible; and disposing of any waste in a way that prevents vermin or disease."

> "Take an active part in at least eight nights away as a Scout. Four of the nights should be camping." / "Explore the environment of your camp and make sure you know where everything is. Respect the environment you are in and, at the end of the camp, leave the site as you found it." / "Lead, or help to lead, a group of Scouts to set up a well-organised site."

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with a residential weekend (typically Friday evening to Sunday afternoon).

## Running order (example, Friday evening – Sunday)

| When | Block |
| --- | --- |
| Friday, arrival | Arrive, patrols lay out and pitch their site using this term's Site Layout & Design thinking |
| Friday evening | Informal games, campfire chat |
| Saturday morning | Shop-bought ingredients organised and stored correctly per this term's planning session |
| Saturday afternoon/evening | Patrols cook and serve their two-course menu; clear up afterwards |
| Sunday morning | Strike camp, patrol chores, pack away |
| Sunday, before departure | Debrief circle |

## Session detail

### Friday: arrival and settle-in

Patrols lay out their site — sleeping tents, food/equipment store, fire/stove area, kitchen and eating area — applying what they worked out in [Site Layout & Design](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/site-layout-design.md). Walk each patrol round the whole site so they know where everything is (the "know where everything is" half of the passive Outdoor Challenge Award requirement).

### Saturday: cook, serve, clear up

Each patrol cooks and serves their planned two-course menu, using the fire-lighting and knife skills from [Fire Safety & Camp Cooking Prep](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/fire-safety-and-camp-cooking.md) — an open fire, a trangia/stove, or a camp kitchen, whichever the patrol planned around. Watch for and prompt on food-waste-minimising choices as they cook (using what's planned, not over-preparing). Immediately after serving, each patrol clears up properly: washing utensils/pots/pans and the cooking area, sorting anything reusable/compostable/recyclable, and disposing of the rest in a way that won't attract vermin. This is the actual evidence for both Chef requirements — note which patrols genuinely managed the full cook-serve-clear-up cycle themselves versus needing heavy leader intervention.

**Materials:** each patrol's own shopping (per their menu plan), cooking equipment (trangia/stove or fire kit), washing-up kit.

### Sunday: strike camp and debrief

Patrols strike and pack away camp, then walk their pitch before leaving — litter picked, ground checked, nothing left that wasn't there Friday (the "leave the site as you found it" half of the passive Outdoor Challenge Award requirement). Finish with a short debrief circle: what worked about their menu, what they'd change next time.

## Notes

- Needs a completed camp risk assessment and site booking well ahead of the term, plus a fire risk assessment (already covered by [Fire Safety & Camp Cooking Prep](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/fire-safety-and-camp-cooking.md) if that session's risk assessment is still valid for this site).
- Counts towards this term's Nights Away staged progression, and — combined with every other term's Camp — towards the Outdoor Challenge Award's Nights Away requirement.
- This camp resolves the well-organised-site requirement in full, since patrols specifically planned for it this term — a stronger, more deliberate sign-off than a camp where site layout was never taught first.
- Leaders should note which patrols cooked, served, and cleared up their full two-course menu as the primary evidence for the Chef Activity Badge.
