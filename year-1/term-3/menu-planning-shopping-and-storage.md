# Menu Planning, Shopping & Storage

**Term:** Year 1, Term 3 — Fire & Cooking

**Fits the term because:** it's the planning groundwork every patrol needs before they can actually cook anything at this term's Camp — the three requirements here are all "before you cook" knowledge, distinct from the hands-on cooking itself.

**Badge focus:** Chef Activity Badge requirements — planning a menu, shopping and transport, and food storage/hygiene.

> "Plan a menu for a weekend event for between four and six people. Include the quantities you'll need and any dietary needs of the group, and think about how your choices can help to minimise food waste."

> "Show how and where to shop for the food and the best way to transport it."

> "Show how to store food properly and hygienically to prevent food poisoning and keep it fresh for longer. Think about what you might need to consider if you're using an indoor kitchen or a camp kitchen."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Plan your menu (40 min)

In patrols of 4–6, plan a two-course menu for this term's Camp. Work out quantities per person, check for any dietary needs or allergies in the patrol (confirm these are accurate — cross-reference with whatever's already on file from consent forms), and discuss at least one way the menu choice reduces food waste (buying the right quantity, using a whole ingredient across both courses, choosing something that reheats or keeps well). Each patrol writes up their final menu and shopping list.

**Materials:** [menu planning template](../../resources/menu-planning-template.md), pens.

### Shopping & transport (30 min)

Discuss where patrols would actually shop for their planned menu (supermarket, local shop, market) and why — price, what's actually needed versus over-buying, supporting local suppliers where practical. Then cover transport: keeping cold items cold until they're needed (a cool box or bag, buying perishables last), keeping raw meat separate from other food, and not overpacking a car boot or rucksack so things get crushed.

### Storing it properly (30 min)

Cover safe food storage: keeping raw and ready-to-eat food separate, correct fridge/cool-box temperatures, use-by versus best-before dates, and what changes between an indoor kitchen (a fridge and freezer available) and a camp kitchen (cool boxes, ice packs, food used in the right order so nothing spoils). Tie this directly to what each patrol just planned to buy — what needs to be eaten first, what can wait.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Keep each patrol's finished menu and shopping list — they'll need it for real at [Camp](camp.md), where the cooking and serving actually happens.
- Leaders should note engagement with all three blocks as the evidence for these requirements — the actual test of whether the planning was any good happens at Camp.
