# Term Kickoff & Safety Briefing

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills
**Fits the term because:** sets up patrols, the term's badge goals, and the outdoor safety expectations that every later campcraft session depends on.
**Badge focus:** no specific Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — this session lays the safety groundwork the rest of the term builds on, and introduces the requirements Scouts will be working towards.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:25 | 15 min | Patrols & term reveal |
| 0:25–0:40 | 15 min | Icebreaker: human knot |
| 0:40–1:00 | 20 min | Outdoor safety induction |
| 1:00–1:20 | 20 min | Outdoor Challenge Award treasure map |
| 1:20–1:50 | 30 min | Campsite party games |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags up, opening.

### Patrols & term reveal (15 min)

Reform patrols for the year and vote in Patrol Leaders and Assistant Patrol Leaders (a show of hands per patrol, run in parallel so it takes minutes not an age). While that's happening, unveil a large "term map" poster on the wall — the 12 sessions drawn as stops on a trail leading to a campfire and a badge at the end. Each patrol gets a small flag or shield to decorate later in the term as they hit milestones. This is the visual anchor you'll refer back to all term, so make it something that survives being pinned up and taken down twelve times.

**Materials:** term trail poster, blank patrol flags/shields, pens.

### Icebreaker: human knot (15 min)

[Try the human knot game](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/try-the-human-knot-game/) — patrols stand in a circle, reach across and grab two different hands, then untangle themselves into a circle without letting go. Run it patrol vs. patrol as a race; the physical problem-solving and forced cooperation is a good primer for the teamwork the term's practical sessions will need.

### Outdoor safety induction (20 min)

This is the one that needs to land properly, so don't do it as a straight talk — run it as a spot-the-mistake performance:

1. **The bad example skit (5 min).** Two leaders act out a deliberately terrible mini-scene: one leader wanders off from the group to "look at something cool" without saying anything, doesn't check in, and gets "lost." Ham it up — Scouts love watching adults get it wrong.
2. **Spot the mistake (5 min).** Ask patrols to shout out everything the leader did wrong. Write their answers on a whiteboard as they land: didn't tell anyone, went off alone, no buddy, didn't have a whistle, etc. You're building the rules from what they already noticed, not lecturing them.
3. **Buddy check drill (5 min).** Explain the buddy system, then rehearse it as a game: call out "Buddy check!" at random over the next few sessions (and on camp) — Scouts must grab their buddy's arm and both shout "Here!" within five seconds. Practice it twice now so it's already a reflex before session 4's outdoor session.
4. **STOP mnemonic with actions (5 min).** Teach what to do if lost as a physical chant: **S**top (freeze on the spot), **T**hink (tap your head), **O**bserve (hand shielding eyes, look around), **P**lan (thumbs up). Get the whole room doing the actions together, then cold-call a couple of Scouts to run through it solo for a cheer.

**Materials:** whiteboard/flip chart, a whistle.

### Outdoor Challenge Award treasure map (20 min)

Reveal the Outdoor Challenge Award requirements as items to "collect" along the term trail poster from the earlier block — print each requirement onto a small card styled like a map marker (pitch a tent, cook on a fire, campsite hygiene, and so on) and pin the first one (safety awareness, just covered) onto the poster now, in front of them, so they see progress happening immediately. Each patrol then writes their own one-sentence term goal on a postcard, seals it in an envelope, and hands it in — these get opened again in the final session as a "did we get there?" moment.

**Materials:** requirement cards, postcards/envelopes, pins or Blu Tack.

### Campsite party games (30 min)

Run three short rotating games in patrols, roughly 10 minutes each, all with a light camp theme:

- **Tent-peg horseshoes** — toss beanbags or rubber tent pegs into hoops from a set distance, patrol relay for points.
- **Camp charades** — Scouts act out camp scenarios (pitching a tent in a gale, burning the toast, a leaky wellington) for their patrol to guess.
- **Kim's game camp tray** — a tray of ten camp items shown for 30 seconds, covered, patrols write down as many as they remember. This one's a deliberate taster for the first aid kit version of the game in session 7.

**Materials:** beanbags/tent pegs, hoops, camp item tray.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- The Outdoor Challenge Award requirements referenced across this term's sessions are drawn from the [Scouts Outdoor Challenge Award](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/awards/outdoor/) page — check the live page before formally signing anything off, since the Scout Association updates requirement wording from time to time.
- Keep the term trail poster and requirement cards up in the meeting place for the rest of the term so Scouts can see their own progress.
