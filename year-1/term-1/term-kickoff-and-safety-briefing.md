# Term Kickoff & Safety Briefing

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** sets up patrols, the term's badge goals, and the outdoor safety expectations that every later campcraft session depends on.

**Badge focus:** no specific Outdoor Challenge Award requirement this session — it lays the safety groundwork the rest of the term builds on, and gives a first look at the requirements Scouts will be working towards.

Like every term's opening session, this one is deliberately light on planning — a quick welcome and reform of patrols, then straight into games. The one addition this term is the safety induction, since it's foundational for everything that follows.

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags up, opening.

### Patrols reformed & term overview (10 min)

Reform patrols for the year and vote in Patrol Leaders and Assistant Patrol Leaders — a show of hands per patrol, run in parallel so it takes minutes, not an age. While that settles, give a quick spoken rundown, a few minutes and no props needed: this term's theme (Campcraft & Outdoor Skills), what we'll be doing (tent pitching, hygiene, fire and cooking, a weekend camp), and the badge on offer (the Outdoor Challenge Award, Part 1). Keep it short — the detail lands session by session, this is just enough for everyone to know what term they're in.

### Icebreaker: human knot (15 min)

[Try the human knot game](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/try-the-human-knot-game/) — patrols stand in a circle, reach across and grab two different hands, then untangle themselves into a circle without letting go. Run it patrol vs. patrol as a race; the physical problem-solving and forced cooperation is a good primer for the teamwork the term's practical sessions will need.

### Outdoor safety induction (20 min)

No performing required — this runs as a set of small-group card tasks with the Scouts doing the work, not the leaders:

1. **Hazard spotting cards (8 min).** Give each patrol 4–5 printed scenario cards, one bad-practice situation per card: "wanders off from the group to look at something, doesn't tell anyone," "goes exploring at dusk with no torch," "ignores the weather forecast and heads out without a coat," "leaves the whistle/kit behind." Patrols discuss each card for a minute and note what's wrong and what should happen instead, then one patrol reads back each card and its answer to the room. You're building the rules from what they work out, not lecturing them.
2. **Buddy check drill (6 min).** Explain the buddy system in plain terms, then rehearse the check itself as a simple call-and-response, like a register: call out "Buddy check!" — Scouts grab their buddy's arm and both shout "Here!" within five seconds. Run it two or three times now so it's already familiar before you start using it for real over the rest of the term (and on camp).
3. **STOP card sequencing (6 min).** Hand each patrol a shuffled set of four cards, each with one word and a one-line description: **S**top ("stay where you are"), **T**hink ("what do you know, what do you need"), **O**bserve ("look around for a landmark or a way back"), **P**lan ("decide what to do, then do it"). Patrols race to put them in the right order, then the room reads the sequence aloud together once. Leave the STOP poster up on the wall as a reference for the rest of the term.

**Materials:** hazard scenario cards (printed, reusable), STOP sequencing cards, a STOP poster for the wall, a whistle for the buddy check.

### Campsite party games (55 min)

Four short rotating games in patrols, roughly 12–15 minutes each, all with a light camp theme — no prep beyond what's already lying around the store cupboard:

- **Tent-peg horseshoes** — toss beanbags or rubber tent pegs into hoops from a set distance, patrol relay for points.
- **Camp charades** — Scouts act out camp scenarios (pitching a tent in a gale, burning the toast, a leaky wellington) for their patrol to guess.
- **Kim's game camp tray** — a tray of ten camp items shown for 30 seconds, covered, patrols write down as many as they remember. This one's a deliberate taster for the first aid kit version of the game later in the term.
- **Camp relay tag** — a simple team tag/relay game with a camp-themed forfeit or bonus for the losing patrol (a silly camp song, an impression, and so on).

**Materials:** beanbags/tent pegs, hoops, camp item tray.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.
