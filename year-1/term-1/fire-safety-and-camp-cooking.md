# Fire Safety & Camp Cooking Prep

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** fire and cooking skills are the last big practical building block before the weekend camp, tying together the safety and hygiene habits built in [Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness](outdoor-safety-and-risk-awareness.md) and [Camp Hygiene & Food Safety](camp-hygiene-and-food-safety.md).

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — fire/stove and cooking, plus axe/saw/knife safety.

> "Prepare and light an open fire or set up a suitable stove. Use it to prepare, cook and serve a meal safely."

> "Show how to use an axe, saw or knife safely. You can choose any or all of these tools."

This session also covers two of the "complete at least four" optional extras:

> "Take part in a campfire or other entertainment."

> "Cook a backwoods meal with the group."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Good fire, bad fire (15 min)

Build (or photograph in advance) two example fire lays — one done properly (cleared ground, sensible size, water and a bucket of sand nearby, built away from tents and overhanging branches) and one done badly (built on grass, too big, too close to the tent line, no water on hand). Walk the group past both — or show photos if building two fires isn't practical — and get them to call out the differences before you confirm the rules: safe distance from tents, water/extinguishing method on hand at all times, one person in charge of the fire, how to put it out properly afterwards.

### Knife safety & feathering sticks (15 min)

A short, concrete safety brief before anyone picks up a blade: fixed-blade knives only at this age, always cut away from the body, keep a "blood circle" of arm's-length-plus clear space around you before drawing the knife, carry it sheathed and hand it over closed or blade-first-away, sit down to cut rather than standing. Demonstrate feathering a stick — thin curls cut part-way down a piece of kindling to help it catch — then let each Scout have a supervised go, one at a time, with a leader at each knife. The feathered sticks go straight into the fire in the next block, so this isn't a standalone skill, it directly helps light it.

**Materials:** one fixed-blade knife per Scout (or enough to rotate through small groups), kindling-sized sticks, gloves if the group's policy requires them.

### Backwoods biscuits (30 min)

[Backwoods biscuits](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/backwoods-biscuits/) — patrols build and light a star fire (logs arranged like spokes, pushed inward as they burn, which is a naturally good fit for a shorter meeting slot since it burns at a controlled rate) using the feathered sticks just cut, then use it to cook the campfire biscuit dough wrapped around a stick. Assign one fire-lighter and one fire-watcher per patrol at all times, rotating through the patrol so everyone gets a turn at each role. This is also the "cook a backwoods meal with the group" optional extra.

**Materials:** firewood, matches/firesteels, biscuit dough ingredients, sticks, water/fire bucket.

### Cook it, serve it safely (20 min)

Building directly on the fire just lit, patrols prepare and cook one more simple item (a sausage, a piece of bread, a twist of dough) and then serve it round the patrol properly — plates or clean hands only, no double-dipping, food covered while waiting. This is the actual "prepare, cook, and serve a meal safely" requirement in action, so watch for and correct any hygiene slip-ups (the ones from the previous session's hand-washing game) as they cook.

**Materials:** simple cooking ingredients, plates/tongs.

### Campfire entertainment taster (15 min)

Teach the room one simple camp song or call-and-response chant, then give each patrol five minutes to prepare a 30-second skit or joke to perform at the weekend camp's campfire. This also counts towards the Outdoor Challenge Award's optional-extras list.

### Clear up and recap (5 min)

Douse and check the fire is properly out, tidy the cooking area, and a one-line recap of the safe-distance and water-on-hand rules from earlier.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs an outdoor space cleared for fire lighting, water/fire extinguisher on hand, and a completed fire risk assessment per group policy — plus a knife-use risk assessment covering ratios and supervision for the feathering-sticks block.
- Check the group's own policy on the minimum age/leader-ratio for knife use before this session — some groups require a specific safety talk to be delivered by a nominated person or hold a minimum badge/training requirement for supervising leaders.
- The campfire entertainment and backwoods-meal activities count towards the Outdoor Challenge Award's optional-extras list, alongside the rucksack packing from [Camp Kit & Personal Equipment](camp-kit-and-personal-equipment.md) and the menu planning at [Camp](camp.md) — four extras in total this term.
- Leaders should note who prepared, cooked, and served safely, and who demonstrated safe knife use, during this session — combined with the same skills repeated for real at [Camp](camp.md), this is the evidence for the fire/stove and tool-safety requirements.
