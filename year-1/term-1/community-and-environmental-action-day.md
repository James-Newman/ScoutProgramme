# Community & Environmental Action Day

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Community & Global Citizenship

**Fits the term because:** it's where the term's research and reflection turns into actually doing something for the local community — and, chosen well, ticks off two requirements with one placement rather than two separate activities.

**Badge focus:** World Challenge Award requirements — a day volunteering with a local community service, and an environmental project.

> "Spend a day volunteering with and finding out about a service in your local community."

> "Take an active part in an environmental project."

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with a day (or half-day) placement at a local service. Pick a placement with a genuine environmental angle — a local nature reserve, a community garden or allotment, a council-run litter-pick or tree-planting scheme — and both requirements above are satisfied by the same placement, rather than needing two separate arrangements.

## Running order (example, half-day)

| When | Block |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | Introduction from the host organisation, safety briefing |
| Morning/early afternoon | Volunteering work (litter-picking, planting, habitat maintenance, garden upkeep — whatever the host needs) |
| Partway through | Host talks through what the service does, its challenges, and who relies on it |
| Before departure | Group discussion: what positive impact could the Troop have on this service in future? |

## Session detail

### Choosing and booking a placement

Contact a local nature reserve, wildlife trust, community garden, allotment association, or the local council's environmental team two to three months ahead — a half-day slot for a group of Scouts needs planning in the same way as an External Activity booking. Ask specifically what hands-on task the group can help with; the requirement needs "active part," not a passive tour.

### On the day

Address the badge's own prompt questions directly with the group at some point during the day: what are the service's challenges? Who relies on it? What positive impact could the Troop have on it in the future (a repeat visit, an ongoing partnership, fundraising)? These don't need a separate formal session — working them into a natural pause during the placement (a break, the walk back) is enough.

### Wrap-up

Before everyone leaves, a quick round: one thing they learned about the service, one thing that surprised them about the environmental work involved.

## Notes

- Needs a placement risk assessment and enough leader/adult supervision for the specific task the host organisation sets — follow the group's own safety policy for off-site activities in the same way as [Camp](camp.md) or [External Activity](external-activity.md).
- If no environmentally-linked local service is within reach, the two requirements can be split across two separate arrangements instead (e.g. a food bank for the volunteering requirement, a separate litter-pick or planting session for the environmental requirement) — just note that this uses two of the term's flexibility rather than one.
- Leaders should note engagement with both the practical task and the discussion questions as the evidence for these two requirements.
