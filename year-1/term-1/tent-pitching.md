# Tent Pitching

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** tent pitching is the term's headline campcraft skill, and this is where it's built and proven for real, outdoors, ahead of the weekend camp. A single simple requirement doesn't need a separate theory week and a separate practical week, so both live in this one session — a short recap up front, then straight outdoors.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — pitch and strike a tent. This session is where it's practically demonstrated and signed off.

> "With others, pitch and strike your tent."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Tent parts, ground selection & tool safety recap (20 min)

Keep this brisk — it's a recap and safety brief, not a repeat of a full lesson:

1. **Name that part (8 min).** Hold up tent components one at a time (pole, peg, guy line, groundsheet, flysheet) and get patrols to race to shout the correct name. Quick and competitive rather than a diagram lesson.
2. **Good ground, bad ground (5 min).** Quickfire verbal check: what do we look for when choosing where to pitch? Cold-call answers — flat, not in a dip, away from the fire, guy lines facing into the wind — rather than presenting it yourself.
3. **Tool safety brief (7 min).** A short, concrete brief for mallets and pegs: point end down and away from feet when carrying, one Scout swings at a time with everyone else's hands clear, mallet handed over (not thrown) between Scouts. Demonstrate the "safety triangle" — everyone else stands back at arm's-length-plus while a peg is driven in.

### Pitch a patrol tent (35 min)

[Pitch a patrol tent](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/pitch-a-patrol-tent/) — full outdoor pitch in patrols, on real ground, applying the ground-and-wind checks just covered to choose their spot. Commentate on it like a sports event as they go ("Beaver patrol going for the classic four-corner start, Fox patrol trying something ambitious with the guy lines") — a bit of theatre keeps the energy up over what's otherwise a fairly technical 35 minutes. This is the practical evidence for the Outdoor Challenge Award's pitch-and-strike requirement, so note which Scouts actively pitched (not just stood nearby) as you go round.

**Materials:** one tent per patrol, mallets, ground sheet.

### Learn to pitch a mess tent (25 min)

[Learn to pitch a mess tent](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/learn-to-pitch-a-mess-tent/) — a group task on the larger shared tent, which needs more coordination than a patrol tent. Assign explicit roles before starting (pole team, peg team, guy-line team, caller) and rotate them halfway through so everyone gets a go at the "caller" role of directing the team.

**Materials:** mess tent, mallets.

### Timed strike relay (10 min)

Reverse the process: patrols race to strike (take down) and correctly pack away their patrol tent, competing on both speed and whether it's packed correctly (a badly rolled tent that won't fit its bag doesn't count as done).

### Debrief and sign-off log (10 min)

Quick huddle per patrol: what went well, what was fiddly. Leaders note against each Scout's name whether they've demonstrated pitch-and-strike to a standard you're happy to log towards the Award.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs outdoor space (field, garden, or campsite) and good weather contingency — if postponed, swap with [Site Layout & Design](site-layout-design.md), since that session's design task is paper-based and doesn't need outdoor ground the way this one does.
- Have spare tents rigged nearby in case of breakages so no patrol loses practice time.
- One tent per small group of four to six, as recommended by the Pitch a patrol tent activity guidance — they don't need to match.
