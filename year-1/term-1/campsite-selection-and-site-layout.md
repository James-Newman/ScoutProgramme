# Campsite Selection & Site Layout

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** pitching a single tent is only half the skill — this session scales that up to laying out a whole patrol site, which is what Scouts will actually do at the weekend camp.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — lead, or help lead, a well-organised site.

> "Lead, or help to lead, a group of Scouts to set up a well-organised site. It should include sleeping tents, food and equipment stores, a fire or stove, kitchen and eating area."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Spot the hazard (15 min)

Show 5–6 photos or a short video of real campsites, drawing on [Camping standards](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/camping-standards/) guidance for what to look for. For each, race patrols to shout out hazards before you reveal a checklist (drainage, slope, overhanging branches, distance from the fire circle, nearby water). First patrol to spot the most gets a point — keep the pace quick so it feels like a game, not an inspection.

**Materials:** campsite photos/video.

### Good pitch, bad pitch sort (20 min)

In pairs, Scouts sort a shuffled deck of laminated site photo cards into "good pitch" and "bad pitch" piles against the clock, then swap decks with another pair to check each other's sorting and argue out any disagreements. The disagreements are where the actual learning happens, so leave time for a couple of them to be debated in front of the whole room.

**Materials:** laminated site photo cards (one deck per pair).

### Design your site (35 min)

Give each patrol a large blank grid ("the field") and a set of card counters or stickers representing a sleeping tent, the mess tent, a food/kit store, the fire circle, and the latrine/wash area. Working from the requirement to set up "sleeping tents, food and equipment stores, a fire or stove, kitchen, and eating area," patrols physically place their counters on the grid, reasoning out loud about wind direction, slope, and distance between the fire and the tents. Treat it like a board game rather than a worksheet — get them moving pieces around and arguing about it.

**Materials:** blank grid sheets, counters/stickers per patrol.

### Site inspectors (20 min)

Each patrol presents their layout to the room. One patrol per round plays "site inspector," using a simple scorecard (safety, practicality, tidiness) to give the presenting patrol feedback and a score out of ten, then rotate so every patrol both presents and inspects.

**Materials:** scorecards.

### Weekend camp preview (10 min)

If the actual weekend camp site is known, show real photos or a map of it now and talk through how today's layout thinking will apply there. If it isn't known yet, use this time to answer any lingering questions from the design task instead.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- See [Plan a perfect Scout camp](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/programme-planning-pros/) for further site-planning ideas if patrols need a steer.
- Bring squared paper, a whiteboard, or a printed grid per patrol for the layout task.
