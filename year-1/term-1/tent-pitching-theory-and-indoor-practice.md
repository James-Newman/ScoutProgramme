# Tent Pitching: Theory & Indoor Practice

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills
**Fits the term because:** tent pitching is the term's headline campcraft skill; this session builds the understanding needed before Scouts pitch for real outdoors next week.
**Badge focus:** builds towards the Outdoor Challenge Award requirement to pitch and strike a tent with others — the practical sign-off happens in [Tent Pitching: Outdoor Practical](tent-pitching-outdoor-practical.md).

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Name that part relay (15 min)

Lay out a disassembled tent's components on a table at one end of the hall — poles, pegs, guy lines, groundsheet, flysheet, inner. At the other end, pin up a large unlabelled diagram of a pitched tent. One Scout at a time runs to the table, picks up a component, runs to the diagram, and sticks a label with its name in the right place. Patrol relay for time. This gets the vocabulary embedded physically before anyone touches a real tent.

**Materials:** disassembled tent parts, large tent diagram, sticky labels.

### Learn how to pitch a tent (40 min)

[Learn how to pitch a tent](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/teamwork-tents/) — in groups of four to six, one tent each (they don't need to match), Scouts first explore the parts hands-on, then work as a team to pitch it on the hall floor. Give each group a numbered set of steps out of order on cards; they have to sequence the cards correctly before they're allowed to start pitching, which forces them to think through the process rather than just copying a neighbouring group.

**Materials:** one tent per small group, sequencing cards.

### Pegs and poles ID relay (20 min)

A faster-paced follow-on: patrols race to correctly identify and connect poles/pegs by feel alone, from a bag, blindfolded or with eyes closed — a fun way to check the vocabulary and shapes have actually stuck, not just been read off a diagram.

**Materials:** bags of mixed tent poles/pegs, blindfolds (optional).

### Would you camp here? (15 min)

Show a sequence of 8–10 photos of pitched tents and campsite spots on a screen — some good, some clearly bad (pitched in a dip that floods, guy lines facing downwind, on a slope, too close to the fire). For each, patrols vote thumbs up or down, then a couple of Scouts explain their reasoning before you reveal what actually happened at that spot. This sets up the ground and wind selection knowledge needed for next week's outdoor session.

**Materials:** photo slides/printouts.

### Review and questions (10 min)

Quickfire round: hold up a tent part or a photo, first patrol to correctly name it/call it good-or-bad gets a point. Settle any lingering questions before next week's outdoor practical.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- One tent per small group of four to six, as recommended by the [Pitch a patrol tent](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/pitch-a-patrol-tent/) activity guidance — they don't need to match.
- Leave plenty of floor space between groups so poles can be handled safely indoors.
