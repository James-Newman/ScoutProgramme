# Camp Hygiene & Food Safety

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** hygiene is one of the term's three stated core goals alongside tent pitching and outdoor safety, and it's easy to skip in favour of the more exciting practical skills — this session makes sure it doesn't get missed.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — personal and campsite hygiene.

> "Find out why personal and campsite hygiene is important. What should you do to be hygienic?"

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### The gross-out hook (10 min)

Open with a short, deliberately vivid story (told, not read) about a real camp getting food poisoning from unwashed hands or undercooked food — the more relatable the detail, the better it lands with this age group. Follow with a quick show-of-hands Q&A: who's ever been camping and skipped washing their hands before dinner? No judgement, just setting up why this session matters before any rules get mentioned.

### Clean up your act (30 min)

[Clean up your act](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/clean-up-your-act/) — brush fluorescent lotion onto Scouts' hands, have them wash as normal, then check under a UV light to see what got missed. Run it twice: once with a rushed "camp in a hurry" wash, once with a proper wash, so the visual contrast makes the point better than any explanation could.

**Materials:** UV lamp, fluorescent lotion/powder, washing facilities.

### True or false floor debate (25 min)

Mark one side of the room "true" and the other "false." Read out a series of food hygiene statements drawn from [Scouts food hygiene guidance](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/staying-safe-and-safeguarding/safety/safe-camping-residentials-and-outdoor-skills/guidance-for-food-safety/food-hygiene/) — keeping food covered, separating raw and ready-to-eat, washing hands before handling food — and Scouts physically move to the side they think is correct before you reveal the answer. The physical commitment (you can't quietly sit on the fence) keeps everyone engaged, and it's an easy format to speed up or slow down depending on how the group's doing.

**Materials:** statement list.

### Toilets, rubbish, and waste at camp (20 min)

Talk through scenario cards in patrols — "there's no bin, what do you do with your rubbish," "you need the loo at night, what's the process," "food scraps after dinner" — with each patrol working out and presenting their answer. If space allows, do a quick practical walk-through of how the group actually sets up a wash station or latrine area at camp, rather than leaving it entirely theoretical.

**Materials:** scenario cards.

### Wash kit relay (15 min)

A race rather than a lecture to close: from a jumbled pile of items (some are wash kit — soap, flannel, toothbrush, towel — some are decoys), patrols race to correctly pick out and pack a wash bag. Double-checks that the wash kit from [Camp Kit & Personal Equipment](camp-kit-and-personal-equipment.md) actually made it onto everyone's list.

**Materials:** mixed pile of wash kit items and decoys.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs a UV lamp and fluorescent lotion/powder for the hand-washing game — check with the district or a local supplier if the group doesn't own one.
- This is a good session to also confirm any dietary requirements or allergies ahead of the weekend camp menu planning in [Weekend Camp Preparation](weekend-camp-preparation.md).
