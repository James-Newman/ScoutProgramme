# Litter Pick

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Community & Global Citizenship

**Fits the term because:** it's where the term's research turns into doing something for the community Scouts live in. [Community Research](community-research.md)'s [Environmental Considerations](../../resources/environmental-considerations-wooburn-and-bourne-end.md) briefing already names litter as a recurring local issue a Scout litter-pick could realistically help with, so this session is a direct follow-through rather than a standalone activity. It also asks patrols to make a real planning decision (where will our hour do the most good?) rather than just following a leader around.

**Badge focus:** World Challenge Award requirements — an environmental project, and volunteering with a local community service (an evening, adapted from a day — see Notes).

> "Take an active part in an environmental project."

> "Spend a day volunteering with and finding out about a service in your local community."

This session counts towards both requirements. Requirement 5 (the environmental project) is met as written; requirement 2 is met in an evening rather than a full day, under the Scout Association's note that "requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities." See [Notes](#notes).

This is a standard 2-hour session (10 min welcome + 100 min core + 10 min close), but most of the core time is spent outside the hall in small groups with an adult each. **The pick happens after dark, so every Scout must bring a working torch** (a head torch is best, since their hands will be full with a picker and a bag), and everyone wears a hi-vis vest.

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening. Check every Scout is wearing sturdy closed shoes and a coat for the weather, **and has brought a torch that works** (switch it on to check, and check it's got fresh batteries). Keep a few spare torches and batteries for anyone who has forgotten theirs, and don't let a Scout without a working torch go out. Hand out hi-vis vests as they come in (nobody leaves without one).

Form the litter-pick groups now: **4–5 Scouts per group, never more than 6**, each with one adult. Use patrols as the starting point and split any patrol larger than 6; keep Scouts from the same patrol together where it works, since they already know how each other behaves outside.

### Brief: the service, the rules, the kit (15 min)

**The service (5 min).** Litter in the parish is dealt with by two services: the [Wooburn & Bourne End Parish Council](https://www.wooburnparish.gov.uk/)'s groundstaff, who maintain council-owned land including Wooburn Park, Bourne End Recreation Ground and the Warren Nature Reserve and empty the litter bins across the parish; and Buckinghamshire Council's street cleaning service, which is lending tonight's litter pickers and bags and will collect what the Scouts fill. Cover three questions in a few words each, drawn from the badge's own prompts: what are the challenges of keeping an area clean, who relies on it, and what could the Troop do for it in future. If the Parish Clerk can arrange for one of the groundstaff to pop in for ten minutes and answer Scouts' questions, that is the strongest version of this block; otherwise a leader reads out the three points above.

**The rules (7 min).** Run these as a short question-and-answer with the room, asking the Scouts what they think the rule should be before you tell them:

- **Stay with your adult at all times.** Nobody wanders off, even to grab a piece of litter ten metres away.
- **Pavements, footpaths and marked trails only.** No roads or carriageways, no roads with a speed limit above 30mph, no steep slopes, and no walking along the edge of the river or any other watercourse. Buckinghamshire Council's own litter-pick guidance excludes all of these.
- **Pick up with the picker, not your hands.** Gloves on all the time.
- **Never touch these; tell your adult instead:** needles or syringes, broken glass, anything that looks like chemicals or smells strongly, anything sharp or rusty, dead animals, bagged dog waste.
- **Bags: fill them about two-thirds, tie them off, and don't carry a bag that's too heavy.** Leave full bags at the agreed collection point with your adult.
- **Torch on, vest on, all the time.** Keep your torch pointed at the ground ahead of you, not into other people's eyes or at passing drivers, and stay on the pavements, footpaths and trails in your route.
- **Whistle or shout for your adult** if you're unsure about anything.

**The kit (3 min).** Show what each group is taking: litter pickers, heavy-duty bags, gloves, hi-vis vests, a torch each, the group's route sheet, and the adult's own torch, first aid kit and phone.

**Materials:** litter pickers, heavy-duty bags, gloves, hi-vis vests, torches (all listed in the [overview's material inventory](overview.md#material-inventory)).

### Plan your route (15 min)

Each group gets the **[Litter Pick Route & Tally Sheet](../../resources/litter-pick-route-and-tally-sheet.md)** and a printed map of the pre-approved litter pick zone (see [Before the session](#before-the-session)), with the no-go areas already marked. The challenge: **choose the route you believe will do the most good for the local area in the time you have.**

The sheet gives groups three quick questions to score each candidate spot, so the decision rests on reasons rather than whoever shouts loudest: how much litter is likely to be there, how many people see or use it (a bus stop or the route to school matters more than a dead-end lane), and who or what it affects (a play area, the river, wildlife). Groups can pick one big spot or link several smaller ones, but must be able to explain why.

Hard limits that every route must meet before a leader signs it off:

- It stays **inside the approved zone, avoids every marked no-go area, and keeps to the pavements, footpaths and woodland trails the leaders have recce'd.** Unlit paths are fine, but the zone map shows which trails are in and out.
- It's a **realistic out-and-back or loop**: all groups **turn round at 1:10 wherever they are** and must be **back at the hall by 1:30**, no exceptions. Remind groups that litter-picking is slow, and a route that looks short on a map can easily take 40 minutes.
- Every group writes their **turn-round time and return time on the sheet**, and the adult confirms they have the leader's phone number and a working phone of their own.

A leader checks each route against these points and signs the sheet. Groups leave as soon as they are signed off, so a group that plans quickly gets more time outside.

**Materials:** printed route & tally sheets, printed zone maps, pens.

### Litter pick (50 min)

Groups head out with their adult. The adult stays with the group, carries the first aid kit and a charged phone, keeps an eye on the time, and handles anything on the "never touch" list. The Scouts do the picking, carry the bags and navigate; give the lead to a different Scout partway through.

At 1:10 every group turns back, whatever they have or haven't finished. A leader stays at the hall to receive groups, with a list of who has left, so any group still out at 1:30 is spotted straight away and phoned.

**Materials:** litter pickers, gloves, hi-vis vests, torches, heavy-duty bags, first aid kit and phone (adult), whistles.

### Bag up, count and tally (10 min)

Back at the hall, groups tie off their bags, tally what they picked up by type on the sheet (plastic bottles, cans, food wrappers, cigarette ends, other), and count the bags they filled. Everyone washes or sanitises their hands before doing anything else. Bags go to the council's agreed collection point (see [Before the session](#before-the-session)).

### Reflection: what difference did we make? (10 min)

Each group reports back in one minute: where they went, why they chose it, how many bags they filled and the most common type of litter they found. A leader adds up the bags on a whiteboard so the Troop sees its combined total. Then ask the room the three questions that stand in for the badge's own prompts:

- What surprised you about what you found, and where you found it?
- If we did this again, which routes would you change, and why?
- What could the Troop do next, beyond a one-off pick? (Come back each term, adopt a regular spot, tell the Parish Council what we found, or take the tally to the school or the village.)

Do not turn this into a contest for the most litter or the heaviest bag, since that encourages Scouts to take risks to pick up things they shouldn't.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Before the session

This is the main prep burden of the session; start about **four weeks ahead**. Buckinghamshire Council's page doesn't state a lead time for litter pick equipment, so treat four weeks as a safe starting point and confirm when you ask.

1. **Parish Council first (it knows the ground).** Contact the Parish Clerk at **clerk@wooburnparish.gov.uk** or **01628 522 827**; the Council Offices are at Town Lane, Wooburn Green, High Wycombe, HP10 0PS (office hours are given as 9am–5pm, Monday to Friday). Say the Troop would like to litter pick on a given evening and ask:
   - Which streets and open spaces would benefit most, and which should be avoided (the Council maintains Wooburn Park, Bourne End Recreation Ground and the Warren Nature Reserve)?
   - Whether the Council can host the equipment drop-off and bag collection point, and whether anyone from the groundstaff could say a few words at the start.
   - Whether they hold any litter pickers, gloves or hi-vis of their own. Their website doesn't mention a loan scheme, so don't assume one.
2. **Buckinghamshire Council for the kit.** Request litter pickers and waste bags, and collection of the full bags, through Buckinghamshire Council's [Organise a community litter pick](https://www.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/waste-and-recycling/street-cleaning/organise-a-community-litter-pick-information/) page. Their service lends litter pickers, supplies bags and collects the bagged waste from an agreed point, and can deliver to a parish council. **They don't mention hi-vis vests or gloves**, so those need sourcing separately (see the [overview's material inventory](overview.md#material-inventory)). Returns and questions go through their [bins, waste and recycling contact page](https://www.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/waste-and-recycling/contact-bins-waste-and-recycling/).
3. **Do your own risk assessment, for a night activity.** The Council's page is explicit that you are responsible for the risk assessment for your litter pick; theirs only covers their own staff preparing the equipment. Start from the [Risk assessment](#risk-assessment) below, adapt it to your actual zone, and follow the group's own policy for night activities (ratios, hi-vis and torch requirements), the same as for [Night Hike](night-hike.md).
4. **Recce and define the zone.** Walk the area yourself first. Mark on a printed map a **litter pick zone** (a few streets and open spaces, all reachable on foot within about 15 minutes of the hall) and a set of **no-go areas** (roads above 30mph, anywhere without a footway, steep slopes, the river bank, anything else you spot). Include the footpaths and woodland trails you're happy for groups to use, and rule out any that are too narrow, muddy, steep or remote for a group in the dark. Walk it **at the same time of day as the session** so you see what the paths are really like by torchlight, and mark any trip hazards, low branches or boggy stretches on the map. Print one copy per group.
5. **Recruit adults.** Every group of 4–5 Scouts needs one adult for the whole outing, so a Troop of 36 Scouts in groups of 4–5 needs **8 adults**. Ask parents to volunteer through your usual channel at least three weeks out, and brief them in advance (route rules, the "never touch" list, what to do if a Scout is hurt). Check your group's safeguarding policy for how occasional parent helpers should be supervised and recorded; don't assume that it matches how you'd treat enrolled adults.
6. **Tell Buckinghamshire Council it's after dark.** Its guidance is for litter picking in **daylight hours**, and this session has groups outside from about 0:40 until 1:30 into the session, which in Autumn is after sunset for most of the term. Say so on the request form or when you ring, and ask whether they're content to lend the equipment for an evening pick; don't leave it for them to find out afterwards. Their guidance is advice to you rather than a bar, but it's their equipment and their collection service, and the decision on whether a night pick is safe for your Scouts rests with your risk assessment. If the answer is no, the fallback is to run the pick earlier in the term while there's daylight (sunset is roughly 7:50pm on 1 September, 7:20pm on 15 September, 6:45pm on 1 October and 6:15pm on 15 October in Buckinghamshire; check the exact time).
7. **Consent and kit.** Send parents a note ahead of the evening: what the Scouts will be doing, that it's **after dark and each Scout must bring a working torch** (a head torch is best, with spare batteries), that they need sturdy shoes and a coat, and that the group will supply gloves and hi-vis vests.

## Risk assessment

A starting point, not a finished document: the Council's page is clear that the risk assessment is yours to complete, so adapt this to your actual zone and sign it off under your group's own process (see [Scouts example risk assessments](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/staying-safe-and-safeguarding/risk-assessments/example-risk-assessments/) for example formats). Review it after the recce and again on the evening, in case the weather or the ground has changed.

**Activity:** evening litter pick in small groups, after dark, on local pavements, footpaths and woodland trails.  **People at risk:** Scouts, adult helpers, and members of the public the groups meet.

| Hazard | Who could be harmed | Controls | Check on the night |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Traffic where a route runs beside or crosses a road | Scouts, adults | Hi-vis vests and torches on for everyone; routes use footways only, with no roads over 30mph and no walking on a carriageway; crossings only at points marked on the zone map, with the adult leading; Scouts told to stay on the pavement side when picking up litter | Vest and torch check before leaving |
| Poor visibility on unlit footpaths and woodland trails | Scouts, adults | Every Scout and adult has their own working torch (head torch best) plus spare batteries and torches held by leaders; only trails recce'd at night and marked in the zone are used; groups stay close together, with the adult at the back | Torches switched on and tested at the welcome |
| Trips and falls on uneven ground, roots, mud, low branches | Scouts, adults | Sturdy closed shoes; known trip hazards and boggy stretches marked on the map; Scouts told to walk, not run, and to look ahead rather than at the litter; torches pointed at the ground ahead | Check ground and weather before sending groups out |
| Sharps and hazardous waste (needles, broken glass, chemicals, dead animals, bagged dog waste) | Scouts, adults | "Never touch" list briefed to everyone and printed on the route sheet; litter pickers and gloves used throughout; Scouts tell the adult, who decides what to do and, if needed, reports it to the Council or the police afterwards | Re-brief the list immediately before leaving |
| Manual handling: heavy or overfull bags | Scouts | Heavy-duty bags filled to about two-thirds only and tied off; full bags left at the agreed collection point, not carried far; no lifting bags above waist height | |
| Hygiene: contact with dirty litter | Scouts, adults | Gloves worn throughout; litter pickers used instead of hands; hands washed or sanitised straight after, before any food or drink; any cuts covered | Wipes or sanitiser at the hall |
| Scouts separated from the group or lost | Scouts | Groups of 4–5 Scouts (never more than 6), each with one adult for the whole outing; a stay-together rule; whistles; adult carries a charged phone and the leader's number; routes signed off by a leader with a fixed turn-round (1:10) and return (1:30) time; a leader at the hall holds a list of who has left and phones any group not back at 1:30 | Check phone signal on the route during the recce |
| Strangers or unwanted approaches | Scouts | Adult with each group at all times; groups stay on public routes with other people around where possible; adult handles any conversation; leader's number to hand | |
| Weather: rain, wind, cold | Scouts, adults | Coats and sensible footwear in the parent note; no woodland trails in high winds or storm warnings (falling branches), and cancel or rearrange in severe weather; leaders check the forecast on the day | Forecast check; adapt routes if conditions are poor |
| Injury or illness | Scouts, adults | A first aid kit and charged phone with every adult; the adult knows each Scout's medical needs (medical details held by the leaders); a trained first aider among the leaders at the hall; adult knows the route's address points to give to the emergency services | Collect medical and emergency contact details |
| Watercourses (River Wye and similar) | Scouts | Excluded as no-go areas on the zone map; routes stay away from river banks and water's edge | Check the zone map |
| Adult helpers not known to the group | Scouts | Parent helpers briefed in advance and treated in line with the group's safeguarding policy; each group has at least one adult, and your policy decides whether helpers need pairing with another adult or an enrolled leader | Confirm adult list and numbers before leaving |

**Emergency:** the adult calls 999 for anything serious and then phones the leader at the hall; the leader at the hall, who holds the list of groups and routes, sends help to the group's location.

## Notes

- **Requirement 5 (environmental project) is met as written**: Scouts take an active part in cleaning up the local environment. Leaders should note each Scout's engagement in the planning, the pick and the reflection, as the evidence.
- **Requirement 2 (a day volunteering with a service) is met in an evening, adapted from a day.** The group has chosen to count this session, under the Scout Association's "requirements can be adapted" note. The substance of the requirement is still delivered: volunteering with a real local service (the Parish Council's groundstaff and Buckinghamshire Council's street cleaning) and finding out about it through the brief and the reflection. Leaders should note each Scout's engagement in both as the evidence.
- **Passive-progress check.** This session also counts as one occasion towards the Teamwork Challenge Award's "On at least three separate occasions, be part of a Scout team, where you work together to achieve a goal" (see [`filler-sessions/teamwork-challenge-award.md`](../../filler-sessions/teamwork-challenge-award.md)). It reinforces the Countryside Code's "take your litter home" but doesn't replace [Night Hike](night-hike.md), which remains the standing home for that Outdoor Challenge Award requirement. It's also a real, hands-on conservation activity in the spirit of the Environmental Conservation Activity Badge, but nowhere near the duration that badge asks for, so it's a head start only (see the term [overview](overview.md)).
- **If the weather is poor,** a heavy downpour or storm warning means cancel and rearrange rather than send groups out; light rain is fine with coats. Keep a short indoor fallback to hand (for example, extra reflection and planning for a rearranged date).
