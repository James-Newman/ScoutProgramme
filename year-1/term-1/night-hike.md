# Night Hike

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** applies the buddy system and STOP mnemonic from [Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness](outdoor-safety-and-risk-awareness.md) for real, outdoors, in the dark — and it's this term's Hikes Away opportunity.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — the Countryside Code — plus the term's optional Hikes Away (1) staged-progression opportunity.

> "Understand the three points of the Countryside Code. Show what action you can take to follow the code."

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with an evening/early-night walk, typically 6:30–9:00pm depending on sunset. Treat the running order below as a shape to adjust to local daylight and route length, not a fixed script.

## Running order (example, ~2.5 hours)

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Muster & kit check |
| 0:10–0:30 | 20 min | Route, buddy & Countryside Code brief |
| 0:30–1:30 | 60 min | The hike out |
| 1:30–1:50 | 20 min | Halfway stop |
| 1:50–2:30 | 40 min | The hike back |
| 2:30–2:45 | 15 min | Debrief & collection |

## Session detail

### Muster & kit check (10 min)

Meet at the hall (or the walk's start point), register patrols, and run a quick kit check per Scout — torch with spare batteries, footwear and coat suitable for the forecast, whistle, water. Confirm buddy pairs.

### Route, buddy & Countryside Code brief (20 min)

Walk the route on a map with the whole group before setting off — start point, a landmark or two, the halfway stop, the return leg. Reconfirm the buddy system and STOP mnemonic taught in [Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness](outdoor-safety-and-risk-awareness.md) — this is the first time this term they get used for real, outdoors, in the dark, rather than as a drill.

Since the route crosses real countryside, this is also the natural place for the Countryside Code: leave gates as you find them, stick to paths, take litter home, keep dogs (if any come along) under control, be considerate of other people out at night. Ask the group what they'd do in a couple of quick scenarios (a gate left open, litter on the path) rather than just reading the code out — it sticks better as a "what would you do" than a list.

**Materials:** route map, torches, whistles.

### The hike out (60 min)

Walk the outbound leg in patrols, leaders spread front, middle, and back. The ground-selection judgement from [Campsite Selection](campsite-selection.md) is worth pointing out again here — spotting a good, sheltered halfway stop uses the same thinking.

### Halfway stop (20 min)

A short break at a pre-scouted spot — hot chocolate from a flask, a quiet torch-lit game (a Kim's game with a single torch beam works well in the dark), a head-count.

### The hike back (40 min)

Return leg, ideally a different route or direction so it doesn't feel like a straight there-and-back. Rotate who's leading at the front of each patrol.

### Debrief & collection (15 min)

Head-count, a quick "what went well" round while parents arrive, and a reminder that this counts towards Hikes Away progression.

## Notes

- Needs a pre-walked, risk-assessed route and a completed risk assessment for night activities per group policy — see [Scouts example risk assessments](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/staying-safe-and-safeguarding/risk-assessments/example-risk-assessments/).
- Adult:young person ratios, hi-vis, and torch requirements should follow the group's own safety policy for night activities.
- Counts towards this term's Hikes Away (1) staged progression (see [`data/terms.yaml`](../../data/terms.yaml)).
- Leaders should note engagement with the Countryside Code scenarios as the evidence for that requirement.
