# Camp

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Community & Global Citizenship

**Fits the term because:** every term includes one Camp regardless of theme — it's this term's Nights Away opportunity. Since this is the very first term, patrols haven't had a dedicated campcraft session yet (that's [Year 1, Term 2](../term-2/overview.md)'s focus), so this camp is more leader-supported than a later one would be.

**Badge focus:** none — this is the term's Nights Away (2) staged-progression opportunity, not a World Challenge Award requirement.

> "Take an active part in at least eight nights away as a Scout. Four of the nights should be camping."

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with a residential weekend (typically Friday evening to Sunday afternoon). Adjust the running order to the site and travel involved.

## Running order (example, Friday evening – Sunday)

| When | Block |
| --- | --- |
| Friday, arrival | Arrive, pitch camp with leader support, settle in |
| Friday evening | Informal games, campfire chat |
| Saturday | Patrol-led activities, games, free time |
| Saturday evening | Cook and serve dinner as a patrol; campfire with an international flavour |
| Sunday morning | Strike camp, patrol chores, pack away |
| Sunday, before departure | Debrief circle |

## Session detail

### Friday: arrival and settle-in

Since tent pitching hasn't been formally taught yet this term, leaders take a more hands-on role helping patrols pitch — this is a good early exposure to the skill ahead of next term's dedicated session, not a test of it. Settle in, get everyone oriented to the site.

### Saturday: camp in full swing

Patrol-led activities, games, chores rota — a fairly standard camp day. If it fits naturally, a short activity connecting back to the term's theme works well here (a "flags of the world" patrol quiz, or cooking a dish inspired by a country a patrol researched in [International Issues & Connections](international-issues-and-connections.md)), but this is a nice-to-have, not a requirement.

### Saturday evening: cook, serve, campfire

Patrols cook and serve their evening meal, then a campfire to finish the day. Since the term's had an international/global flavour, songs or short pieces from other countries' Scouting traditions are a fun, low-effort tie-in — the [World Scout Movement](https://www.scout.org/) has plenty to draw on.

### Sunday: strike camp and debrief

Patrols strike and pack away camp (with leader support as needed), then a short debrief circle before parents arrive: what went well, what was hard, one thing each patrol would do differently next time.

## Notes

- Needs a completed camp risk assessment and site booking well ahead of the term — see [Scouts example risk assessments](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/staying-safe-and-safeguarding/risk-assessments/example-risk-assessments/).
- Counts towards this term's Nights Away (2) staged progression (see [`data/terms.yaml`](../../data/terms.yaml)).
- Full campcraft skill-building (tent pitching, site layout, fire safety) is [Year 1, Term 2](../term-2/overview.md)'s job, not this term's — keep expectations for this first camp modest and leader-supported.
