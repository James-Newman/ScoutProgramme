# Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** outdoor safety is the third of the term's stated core goals, and sits alongside tent pitching and hygiene as the foundation for everything Scouts do outdoors this term and beyond. This session also carries the general safety induction (buddy system, what to do if lost) that used to sit in the opening session — it fits better here, alongside the rest of the term's safety content.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — accidents and incidents.

> "Find out what accidents and incidents can happen outdoors or during your camp. Show how you would deal with them."

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Safety induction: hazard cards, buddy check, STOP (20 min)

No performing required — this runs as a set of small-group card tasks with the Scouts doing the work, not the leaders:

1. **Hazard spotting cards (8 min).** Give each patrol 4–5 printed scenario cards, one bad-practice situation per card: "wanders off from the group to look at something, doesn't tell anyone," "goes exploring at dusk with no torch," "ignores the weather forecast and heads out without a coat," "leaves the whistle/kit behind." Patrols discuss each card for a minute and note what's wrong and what should happen instead, then one patrol reads back each card and its answer to the room.
2. **Buddy check drill (6 min).** Explain the buddy system in plain terms, then rehearse the check itself as a simple call-and-response, like a register: call out "Buddy check!" — Scouts grab their buddy's arm and both shout "Here!" within five seconds. Run it two or three times now so it's already familiar for the rest of the term (and on camp).
3. **STOP card sequencing (6 min).** Hand each patrol a shuffled set of four cards, each with one word and a one-line description: **S**top ("stay where you are"), **T**hink ("what do you know, what do you need"), **O**bserve ("look around for a landmark or a way back"), **P**lan ("decide what to do, then do it"). Patrols race to put them in the right order, then the room reads the sequence aloud together once. Leave the STOP poster up on the wall as a reference for the rest of the term.

**Materials:** [hazard scenario cards](../../resources/hazard-spotting-cards.md) (printed, reusable), [STOP sequencing cards](../../resources/stop-sequencing-cards.md), a [STOP poster](../../resources/stop-poster.md) for the wall, a whistle for the buddy check.

### First aid kit Kim's game (25 min)

[First aid kit Kim's game](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/first-aid-kit-catch-up/) — lay out the contents of a first aid kit on a tray, cover it after a set viewing time, and have patrols write down everything they remember. This activity is explicitly designed to run in the weeks leading up to a camp or night away, and this session sits just before the term's [Night Hike](night-hike.md).

**Materials:** first aid kit and contents, tray, cover sheet. Use [`first-aid-kit-checklist.md`](../../resources/first-aid-kit-checklist.md) as the tray contents/answer key.

### Emergency scenario bingo (25 min)

[Emergency scenario bingo](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/emergency-scenario-bingo/) — patrols work through printed accident/incident scenario cards (a fall, a burn, someone feeling unwell) and match each to the correct response on a bingo-style grid. If time is short, swap in [Practice calling 999 for first aid scenarios](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/practice-calling-999-for-first-aid-scenarios/) instead — same requirement, same rough timing, just a different format.

**Materials:** printed scenario cards, bingo grids — the official [Emergency Bingo PDF](https://prod-cms.scouts.org.uk/media/4583/ready-to-respond-emergency-bingo.pdf) has both ready to print, with [Emergency Guess Who](https://prod-cms.scouts.org.uk/media/4584/ready-to-respond-emergency-guess-who.pdf) as an alternative format.

### Weather wear dress-off (20 min)

[Weather wear](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/weather-wear/) — pile up a mix of clothing (waterproofs, sun hats, gloves, t-shirts, wellies) in the middle of the room. Draw a weather card ("torrential rain," "blazing sun," "frost") and race one Scout per patrol to grab and put on the most appropriate items before the others. Funny to watch, and it makes the "dress for the forecast, not the season" point stick better than a discussion would.

**Materials:** mixed clothing pile, [weather cards](../../resources/weather-cards.md).

### Recap quickfire (10 min)

Fire quick questions at the room — "what's in the first aid kit," "what do you do if someone's burnt," "what would you wear in a downpour" — first hand up gets a point. Close by having each Scout mentally note one thing for their own personal safety kit for camp.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- This session sits right before the [Night Hike](night-hike.md) so the buddy system and STOP mnemonic are fresh when they're actually needed outdoors, in the dark.
- Leaders should note engagement with the first aid and scenario activities — combined with how Scouts actually apply the buddy system and STOP mnemonic on the Night Hike and at [Camp](camp.md), this is the evidence for the accidents-and-incidents requirement.
