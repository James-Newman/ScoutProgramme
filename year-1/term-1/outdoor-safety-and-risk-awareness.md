# Outdoor Safety & Risk Awareness

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** outdoor safety is the third of the term's stated core goals, and sits alongside tent pitching and hygiene as the foundation for everything Scouts do outdoors this term and beyond.

**Badge focus:** Outdoor Challenge Award requirement — accidents and incidents.

> "Find out what accidents and incidents can happen outdoors or during your camp. Show how you would deal with them."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Spot the risk |
| 0:20–0:45 | 25 min | First aid kit Kim's game |
| 0:45–1:20 | 35 min | Emergency scenario bingo |
| 1:20–1:40 | 20 min | Weather wear dress-off |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Recap quickfire |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### Spot the risk (10 min)

Show 4–5 photos of outdoor/camp scenes, each with a hidden risk (a tent guy line across a walkway, someone in flip-flops near the fire, a knife left unsheathed on a table). Patrols race to spot and name the risk in each photo — quick, competitive, and a natural lead-in to why checking the weather and the site matters before heading out.

**Materials:** risk-spotting photos.

### First aid kit Kim's game (25 min)

[First aid kit Kim's game](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/first-aid-kit-catch-up/) — lay out the contents of a first aid kit on a tray, cover it after a set viewing time, and have patrols write down everything they remember. This activity is explicitly designed to run in the weeks leading up to a camp or night away, which is exactly where it sits in the term.

**Materials:** first aid kit and contents, tray, cover sheet.

### Emergency scenario bingo (35 min)

[Emergency scenario bingo](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/emergency-scenario-bingo/) — patrols work through printed accident/incident scenario cards (a fall, a burn, someone feeling unwell) and match each to the correct response on a bingo-style grid. If time is short, swap in [Practice calling 999 for first aid scenarios](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/practice-calling-999-for-first-aid-scenarios/) instead — same requirement, role-play format rather than bingo, and arguably more memorable if the group responds well to acting things out.

**Materials:** printed scenario cards, bingo grids.

### Weather wear dress-off (20 min)

[Weather wear](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/weather-wear/) — pile up a mix of clothing (waterproofs, sun hats, gloves, t-shirts, wellies) in the middle of the room. Draw a weather card ("torrential rain," "blazing sun," "frost") and race one Scout per patrol to grab and put on the most appropriate items before the others. Funny to watch, and it makes the "dress for the forecast, not the season" point stick better than a discussion would.

**Materials:** mixed clothing pile, weather cards.

### Recap quickfire (10 min)

Fire quick questions at the room — "what's in the first aid kit," "what do you do if someone's burnt," "what would you wear in a downpour" — first hand up gets a point. Close by having each Scout mentally note one thing for their own personal safety kit for camp.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- The First Aid Kit activity is explicitly designed to run in the weeks leading up to a camp or night away — this slot, roughly two weeks before the weekend camp, is a good fit.
