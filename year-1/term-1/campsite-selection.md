# Campsite Selection

**Term:** Year 1, Term 1 — Campcraft & Outdoor Skills

**Fits the term because:** before a patrol can lay out a whole site, they need to be able to judge whether the ground itself is any good — this session is the prep work for [Site Layout & Design](site-layout-design.md) the following week.

**Badge focus:** no new requirement this session — it builds the judgement that [Site Layout & Design](site-layout-design.md) then applies for the sign-off.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:25 | 15 min | What makes a good campsite |
| 0:25–0:40 | 15 min | Spot the hazard |
| 0:40–1:00 | 20 min | Good pitch, bad pitch sort |
| 1:00–1:35 | 35 min | Field walk: pick your spot |
| 1:35–1:50 | 15 min | Compare and debate |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening.

### What makes a good campsite (15 min)

Short discussion drawing on [Camping standards](https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/camping-standards/) guidance: what do we look for before pitching anywhere — drainage, slope, overhanging branches, distance from the fire circle, nearby water, wind direction. Keep it as a discussion, not a lecture — ask the room first, fill gaps after.

### Spot the hazard (15 min)

Show 5–6 photos or a short video of real campsites. For each, race patrols to shout out the hazards before you reveal a checklist. First patrol to spot the most gets a point — keep the pace quick so it feels like a game, not an inspection.

**Materials:** campsite photos/video.

### Good pitch, bad pitch sort (20 min)

In pairs, Scouts sort a shuffled deck of laminated site photo cards into "good pitch" and "bad pitch" piles against the clock, then swap decks with another pair to check each other's sorting and argue out any disagreements. The disagreements are where the actual learning happens, so leave time for a couple of them to be debated in front of the whole room.

**Materials:** laminated site photo cards (one deck per pair).

### Field walk: pick your spot (35 min)

Take the whole group outside — the meeting field, a garden, or a nearby green space. Each patrol walks the space and picks a spot they'd pitch on for real, then has to justify it to a leader on the spot: why here and not ten metres over there? This is the first time this term the selection judgement gets applied to real ground rather than a photo, feeding straight into next week's [Site Layout & Design](site-layout-design.md).

### Compare and debate (15 min)

Back inside, each patrol reports their chosen spot and reasoning. Where two patrols picked differently for similar ground, get them to argue it out in front of the room — that's where any remaining gaps in judgement usually surface.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Needs some outdoor space for the field walk — if the weather or venue doesn't allow it, swap in a second round of photo/video spotting instead and flag that the group is a session behind on hands-on ground judgement.
- No specific badge requirement is signed off this session — see [Site Layout & Design](site-layout-design.md) for where that happens.
