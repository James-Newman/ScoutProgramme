# Getting Help & Unconscious

**Term:** Year 1, Term 2 — First Aid & Emergency Response

**Fits the term because:** it's the natural starting point for Emergency Aid — before anything else, every Scout needs to know how to get help and how to respond if someone is unconscious.

**Badge focus:** Emergency Aid Staged Activity Badge (Stage 3) requirements — getting help/calling 999, reassuring someone at the scene, and helping someone who is unconscious (breathing or not).

> "Explain to your leader or another adult about how to call 999."

> "Explain how you help someone who: is unconscious; is unconscious and not breathing"

Stage 3 is cumulative with Stages 1 and 2, which also cover "the importance of getting help" and "reassuring someone at the scene of an emergency" — both covered in this session too, since a Scout entering fresh at Stage 3 shouldn't skip anything from the earlier stages.

**This must be taught by an adult with relevant first aid knowledge** — an explicit Stage 3 requirement, not just good practice.

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Getting help & calling 999 (20 min)

Why getting help matters before anything else, then what to actually say on a 999 call — location, what's happened, how many casualties, staying on the line. Practise with a dummy-phone role play in pairs, one Scout as caller, one as the call handler asking the standard questions.

**Materials:** a phone (real but not dialling, or a toy/prop phone) per pair.

### Reassuring someone at the scene (15 min)

What reassurance actually looks like in an emergency — calm voice, staying with the casualty, explaining what's happening, not leaving them alone. Quick paired practice: one Scout plays a distressed casualty, the other reassures them using what they just learned.

### Unconscious — checking response & recovery position (25 min)

Teach checking for response (shout and shake gently, check for a reply) and putting someone into the recovery position. Every Scout should physically practise the recovery position on a partner (softly, on a mat or the floor) under close supervision — this is a genuine hands-on skill, not just theory.

**Materials:** mats or soft flooring for recovery-position practice.

### Unconscious and not breathing (30 min)

Teach the difference this makes — checking for breathing after establishing unresponsiveness, and what to do if someone isn't breathing (call/send for emergency help immediately, per the Scout Association's own guidance for this age group — this session teaches recognition and getting help, not CPR technique itself, which is beyond Stage 3's scope). Talk through the sequence using a training manikin if available, or a clear step-by-step talk-through if not.

**Materials:** a first aid training manikin, if available (borrow from a local first aid provider or St John Ambulance if the group doesn't own one).

### Practice & consolidate (10 min)

Quick round-robin: each Scout talks through the full sequence (get help, reassure, check response, recovery position, check breathing) from memory, prompted by a leader.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Must be delivered or directly supervised by an adult with relevant first aid knowledge — an explicit badge requirement, not a suggestion.
- Recovery position practice needs close supervision and appropriately spaced-out pairs — treat it like any other physical skill session in terms of care taken.
- Leaders should note which Scouts can explain and demonstrate each topic as the evidence for these requirements.
