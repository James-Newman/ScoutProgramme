# Camp

**Term:** Year 1, Term 2 — First Aid & Emergency Response

**Fits the term because:** every term includes one Camp regardless of theme — it's this term's Nights Away opportunity, and (per the standing convention for every term's Camp) the home for two Outdoor Challenge Award requirements that accumulate passively regardless of the term's own focus badge. Unlike Year 1, Term 1's first camp, patrols have now been taught [Tent Pitching](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/tent-pitching.md) and [Campsite Selection](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/campsite-selection.md) earlier this term, so they can pitch largely independently this time, with leaders supervising rather than doing it for them.

**Badge focus:** none for Emergency Aid Stage 3 — but this session is the standing home for two passive Outdoor Challenge Award requirements (see [`filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/README.md`](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/README.md)) and this term's Nights Away (+2) staged-progression opportunity. It's also a real, live setting to keep first aid knowledge fresh — a minor scrape or a patrol member feeling unwell over the weekend is a genuine (if unplanned) opportunity to put this term's Emergency Aid content into practice, not something to script in advance.

> "Take an active part in at least eight nights away as a Scout. Four of the nights should be camping."

> "Explore the environment of your camp and make sure you know where everything is. Respect the environment you are in and, at the end of the camp, leave the site as you found it."

This isn't a standard 2-hour hall session — it replaces one week's meeting with a residential weekend (typically Friday evening to Sunday afternoon). Adjust the running order to the site and travel involved.

## Running order (example, Friday evening – Sunday)

| When | Block |
| --- | --- |
| Friday, arrival | Arrive, patrols pitch camp largely independently (leaders supervising, not doing it for them) |
| Friday evening | Informal games, campfire chat |
| Saturday | Patrol-led activities, games, free time |
| Saturday evening | Cook and serve dinner as a patrol; campfire |
| Sunday morning | Strike camp, patrol chores, pack away |
| Sunday, before departure | Debrief circle |

## Session detail

### Friday: arrival and settle-in

Since patrols have now been taught tent pitching and campsite selection earlier this term, let them pitch with only light supervision — step in only if something's genuinely unsafe, not to correct minor technique issues. Once camp is up, walk each patrol round the whole site — where the toilets are, the site boundary, the nearest water point, anything to avoid — so they genuinely know where everything is before dark. That's the "know where everything is" half of the passive Outdoor Challenge Award requirement above.

### Saturday: camp in full swing

Patrol-led activities, games, chores rota — a fairly standard camp day.

### Saturday evening: cook, serve, campfire

Patrols cook and serve their evening meal, then a campfire to finish the day.

### Sunday: strike camp and debrief

Patrols strike and pack away camp — largely independently again, building on the timed strike relay from [Tent Pitching](../../filler-sessions/outdoor-challenge-award/tent-pitching.md) — then walk their pitch before leaving: litter picked, ground checked, nothing left that wasn't there Friday. That's the "leave the site as you found it" half of the passive Outdoor Challenge Award requirement. Finish with a short debrief circle before parents arrive.

## Notes

- Needs a completed camp risk assessment and site booking well ahead of the term — see [Scouts example risk assessments](https://www.scouts.org.uk/volunteers/staying-safe-and-safeguarding/risk-assessments/example-risk-assessments/).
- Counts towards this term's Nights Away (+2) staged progression (see [`data/terms.yaml`](../../data/terms.yaml)), and — combined with every other term's Camp — towards the Outdoor Challenge Award's Nights Away requirement.
- Bring a basic first aid kit and make sure at least one adult on camp is confident with this term's Emergency Aid content — not because anything is expected to go wrong, but because it's genuinely useful given what's just been taught.
