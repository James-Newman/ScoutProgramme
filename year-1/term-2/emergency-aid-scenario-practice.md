# Emergency Aid Scenario Practice

**Term:** Year 1, Term 2 — First Aid & Emergency Response

**Fits the term because:** consolidates all ten of Emergency Aid Stage 3's cumulative topics before the badge is signed off, giving every Scout a genuine chance to demonstrate what they've learned across the term's three teaching sessions rather than being assessed cold.

**Badge focus:** Emergency Aid Staged Activity Badge (Stage 3) — consolidation and evidence-gathering across all cumulative requirements, not new content.

This session doesn't introduce anything new — it's a scenario-card circuit covering everything from [Getting Help & Unconscious](getting-help-and-unconscious.md), [Bleeding, Burns & Choking](bleeding-burns-and-choking.md), and [Heat, Cold & Breathing](heat-cold-and-breathing.md).

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–1:40 | 90 min | Scenario circuit |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Debrief |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Explain the circuit format.

### Scenario circuit (90 min)

Set up stations around the room, each with a scenario card describing one emergency (unconscious and breathing, unconscious and not breathing, bleeding, a burn, choking, heat exhaustion, hypothermia, an asthma attack). Patrols rotate through in small groups, drawing a card at each station and talking through — and where safe to do so, physically demonstrating — the right response (getting help, reassuring, and the specific first aid action). A leader at each station (or circulating) checks the response and gives feedback. Same choking caveat as [Bleeding, Burns & Choking](bleeding-burns-and-choking.md) applies: talk through and explain the choking response, don't practise back blows/abdominal thrusts hands-on.

**Materials:** a set of 8 scenario cards (one per emergency covered this term), any props reused from the three teaching sessions (bandages, a manikin if available, a demo inhaler).

### Debrief (10 min)

Quick round: which scenario did each patrol find hardest, and why. Address any gaps a leader noticed during the circuit.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home, and a note that badge presentation happens at [End of Term Fun & Games](end-of-term-fun-and-games.md).

## Notes

- Must be delivered or directly supervised by an adult with relevant first aid knowledge, same as the term's other Emergency Aid sessions.
- This session is the primary evidence-gathering point for the whole badge — leaders should track each Scout's performance across all 8 scenarios here, alongside what was already noted in the three teaching sessions.
- Worth checking here whether any Scout already holds a first aid award from a recognised provider (British Red Cross, St John Ambulance) covering this syllabus — they can automatically get this badge without needing the circuit, per the badge's own alternative pathway.
