## Badge requirement

Source: [Emergency Aid Staged Activity Badge, Stage 3](https://www.scouts.org.uk/staged-badges/emergency-aid/stage-3) — check the live page before formally signing anything off, since requirement wording is occasionally updated by the Scout Association.

> "You will need to take part in around two to three hours of training, which should be taught by an adult with relevant knowledge."

> "Explain to your leader or another adult about how to call 999."

> "Explain how you help someone who: is unconscious; is unconscious and not breathing; is bleeding; has heat exhaustion; has hypothermia; is choking; has a burn; is having an asthma attack"

Stage 3 explicitly requires "around two to three hours of training" — likely two sessions rather than one, budgeted properly rather than rushed, per the discussion that led to choosing this stage over Stage 1 or 2. Its own requirement list is cumulative with Stages 1 and 2 (calling 999, unconscious, bleeding, reassuring), so a Scout entering fresh isn't skipping anything as long as the training genuinely covers the full list above.

There's also an alternative pathway worth knowing about: "If you hold a first aid award covering this or a similar syllabus from a recognised first aid provider (such as the British Red Cross or St John Ambulance) you can automatically get this badge" — worth checking whether any Scouts already qualify this way before planning full sessions.
