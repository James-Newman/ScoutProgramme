# Bleeding, Burns & Choking

**Term:** Year 1, Term 2 — First Aid & Emergency Response

**Fits the term because:** the second of the term's Emergency Aid sessions — moves from responsiveness/breathing onto three common injuries a Scout is likely to actually encounter.

**Badge focus:** Emergency Aid Staged Activity Badge (Stage 3) requirements — helping someone who is bleeding, has a burn, or is choking.

> "Explain how you help someone who: is bleeding; has a burn; ... is choking"

**This must be taught by an adult with relevant first aid knowledge** — an explicit Stage 3 requirement, not just good practice.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:40 | 30 min | Bleeding |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Bleeding (30 min)

Teach direct pressure and dressing a wound. Every Scout practises applying a dressing to a partner's arm (over clothing, no real wounds needed) — a roll of bandage and some role-play "blood" (red wool, ribbon, or just imagination) works fine.

**Materials:** bandages/dressings, one set per pair.

### Burns (30 min)

Teach cooling a burn under cool running water (or the closest practical equivalent in a hall — a bowl of cool water) for the right amount of time, and covering it loosely once cooled. Discuss what not to do (no ice, no butter or creams, don't burst blisters).

**Materials:** a bowl of cool water per patrol (demonstration only, no real burns involved), cling film or a clean, non-fluffy dressing to demonstrate covering.

### Choking (40 min)

Teach recognising choking and the sequence of back blows and abdominal thrusts. **Do not have Scouts practise back blows or abdominal thrusts on each other** — this is demonstrated by the qualified trainer on a manikin (if available) or talked through step-by-step with the group observing, not practised hands-on between Scouts. Scouts should be able to explain and recognise the sequence, not necessarily perform it on a person by the end of this session.

**Materials:** a choking-demonstration manikin, if available.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Must be delivered or directly supervised by an adult with relevant first aid knowledge — an explicit badge requirement, not a suggestion.
- The choking section is deliberately demonstration-only rather than hands-on-partner practice — abdominal thrusts and back blows carry real injury risk if practised carelessly between young people, unlike bandaging or recovery position.
- Leaders should note which Scouts can explain and demonstrate each topic as the evidence for these requirements.
