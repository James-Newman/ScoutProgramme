# Adventure Challenge Award

**Badge:** [Scouts Adventure Challenge Award](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/awards/adventure/) — mostly passive, with one reflection session.

**Why this works as a filler:** the badge's first requirement — "take part in four different adventurous activities," at least two of them new and tried on at least two separate occasions — accumulates automatically via every term's standing External Activity slot. No dedicated session is needed for that part at all. This session delivers the three reflective requirements, which need real facilitation, once a Scout has enough External Activities behind them to actually reflect on.

**When to run this:** not before at least two or three different External Activities have happened — ideally once a Scout has settled on one activity they'd like to go deeper on.

**Requirements this session covers:**

> "Show how you have developed your skill and expertise in one of these activities." / "Show that you know the safety issues involved, and that you can use any equipment needed for the activity safely."

> "Learn about any environmental issues caused by your activity." / "Take steps to reduce any harm to the environment."

> "Research other ways you can take part, or develop your skills, in your chosen activities." / "Follow up your research with action."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Tally check |
| 0:20–0:45 | 25 min | Choose your activity & reflect on skill/safety |
| 0:45–1:15 | 30 min | Environmental impact & action |
| 1:15–1:45 | 30 min | Research & follow-up action |
| 1:45–1:50 | 5 min | Share |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Tally check (10 min)

Quick round: which External Activities has each Scout tried so far? Check progress against the "four different activities" requirement — at least two of them new to that Scout, each of those two tried on at least two separate occasions. If someone's short, flag which future External Activity bookings would help close the gap (repeating a favourite provider counts, as long as it's one of the "new" ones being repeated for a second occasion).

### Choose your activity & reflect on skill/safety (25 min)

Each Scout picks one of their activities to go deeper on — usually whichever they enjoyed most or want to keep doing. In small groups or one-to-one with a leader, they talk through: how has their skill in this activity actually improved since they started? What safety issues are involved, and what equipment do they need to use safely? This needs to go far enough that a leader can genuinely sign off "knows the safety issues, uses equipment safely" — not just a vague "yeah it's fine."

### Environmental impact & action (30 min)

Using whatever research tools are available, each Scout finds out about one real environmental issue connected to their chosen activity (litter or erosion from hillwalking, fuel use in powered watersports, wildlife disturbance from paddling near nesting areas, and so on), then commits to and actually carries out one concrete step to reduce harm — a "leave no trace" habit adopted from now on, or something bigger if the activity allows it. "Take steps to reduce harm" needs a genuine action, not just an idea — worth checking back in on it at a later session if it's ongoing.

### Research & follow-up action (30 min)

Each Scout researches one way to take their chosen activity further — a local club, a badge or qualification, a more advanced course, a way to practise more often — then follows up with one real action this term: emailing a club, signing up for a taster session, asking a leader about running the activity again. As with the previous block, this needs a genuine follow-up action, not just a plan on paper.

### Share (5 min)

Quick round: one thing each Scout is doing differently as a result of today.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- This session only makes sense after real exposure to a few adventurous activities has already happened via External Activity — don't run it as someone's very first taste of the badge.
- Leaders should note each Scout's chosen specialism activity and their two genuine actions (environmental, further-participation) as the evidence for the reflective requirements.
- The "four activities, two new, two occasions each" tally is best tracked centrally (e.g. Online Scout Manager) across terms, since it spans however many terms it takes to accumulate — this repo doesn't track individual Scout progress.
