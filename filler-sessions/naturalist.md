# Naturalist Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Naturalist Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/nature/)

**Why it's a good filler:** every requirement can run as a single visit to "a suitable and safe location" — the badge doesn't mandate anywhere special, just somewhere safe to explore nature. This assumes the meeting place has adjacent green space (a field, garden, or nearby park), which this programme already relies on for other sessions.

**Adaptation needed:** none, provided a nearby green space is available. If it isn't, this session doesn't fit as a filler — it depends on that access.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> "Explore and connect with nature by visiting a suitable and safe location. Identify different plants and animals, and record what you find."
>
> "Plan how you'll protect, care for and respect the environment during your visit."
>
> "Find out more about a plant, animal or another natural thing that you discovered."
>
> "Share what you've discovered and learned with someone else."
>
> "Explore how human activity or land management affects wildlife, either at your chosen location or more widely."
>
> "Think about your experience in nature and decide on one thing you'd like to do next."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:45 | 35 min | Explore, respect & record |
| 0:45–1:05 | 20 min | Find out more |
| 1:05–1:25 | 20 min | Share what you found |
| 1:25–1:40 | 15 min | Human impact |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Reflect & decide what's next |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Explore, respect & record (35 min)

Before heading out, a quick two-minute brief covers how to protect and respect the space: stay on paths where possible, don't disturb wildlife or damage plants, take litter away, be mindful of anyone else using the space — that's the "plan how you'll protect, care for and respect the environment" requirement, folded in here rather than as a separate block. Then patrols explore the green space, identifying and recording (written notes, sketches, or photos) as many different plants and animals as they can find.

**Materials:** notebooks/paper, pens, field guide or plant/bird identification app if available (not essential).

### Find out more (20 min)

Back inside, each Scout picks one thing they found — a plant, insect, bird, type of habitat, or even something like soil or a rock formation — and finds out more about it, using a book, phone, or whatever reference material is to hand.

**Materials:** internet access or reference books/field guides.

### Share what you found (20 min)

Each Scout shares what they discovered with someone else — a short talk, a quick sketch held up and explained, or a two-minute description to their patrol. Keep the format loose; the requirement is sharing, not a formal presentation.

### Human impact (15 min)

Group discussion: how does human activity affect the wildlife at the chosen location, or more widely? Local examples work best — litter, dog walking, building development, a local conservation project or wildlife group if anyone knows of one — but general examples (climate, habitat loss, plastic pollution) work too if nothing local comes to mind.

### Reflect & decide what's next (10 min)

Each Scout takes a moment to think about the experience and names one thing they'd like to do next — visit somewhere new, learn more about a specific animal, get involved in a local conservation activity. No need to act on it this session; naming it is the requirement.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Materials

Notebooks/paper, pens, an identification guide or app (optional but helpful), whatever reference material is available for the "find out more" block.

## Notes

- Weather-dependent for the outdoor block — if it's genuinely unsafe to go outside, this session doesn't have a clean indoor substitute (unlike some of the others), since "visiting a suitable location" is central to the badge.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities.
