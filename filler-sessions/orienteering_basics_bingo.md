# UK Scouts Orienteering Basics & Bingo

**Aim:** Orienteering Basics, Ahead of the Big O  
Scouts should leave able to orientate a map, identify the main compass directions, understand the idea of a bearing, and recognise a small set of useful map/orienteering terms.

## Session Details

### 1. Welcome & Flag (10 mins)

### 2. Orienteering Fundamentals (20 mins)
*Materials: OS Maps and Compasses, for scouts to have to hand and try.*

1. **Orientate the map**
   - The top of a map is normally north.
   - Put the map flat. Use the compass to find north, then turn the map so north on the map points north in real life.
   - *Simple message:* turn the map to fit the ground.

2. **Compass basics**
   - Red end of the magnetic needle points north.
   - $N = 0^\circ / 360^\circ$, $E = 90^\circ$, $S = 180^\circ$, $W = 270^\circ$.
   - Briefly introduce NE, SE, SW, and NW.

3. **Set a direction from a map**
   - Place the long edge of the compass from where you are to where you want to go.
   - Turn the compass housing so its north lines up with north on the map.
   - Lift the compass. Turn yourself until the red needle lines up with north on the housing ("red in the shed").
   - The direction-of-travel arrow now points the way to go.

4. **Map symbols**
   Get them to find some:
   - **Footpath:** A green dashed line on OS Explorer maps. Public rights of way are generally shown in green.
   - **Road:** Coloured solid lines. The colour and width depend on the type of road. Motorways are blue; major roads are generally red/orange; minor roads are narrower.
   - **Building:** Usually a small solid or outlined block/shape, showing the building's footprint.
   - **Woodland:** An area containing green tree/vegetation symbols.
   - **Water:** Blue lines or areas. Rivers, streams, lakes, and ponds are shown in blue.
   - **Contour lines:** Thin brown/orange lines joining places of the same height. Close together = steep; further apart = gentler slope.

5. **Grid Reference**
   - Grid references tell you where something is on a map.
   - OS maps are divided into numbered grid squares.
   - Read the numbers along the bottom first, then up the side.
   - *Easy way to remember:* "Along the corridor, then up the stairs."

6. **Golden rule**
   - Before moving, ask: *Where am I? Where am I going? What should I see on the way?*

> **QUICK CHECK:** Ask everyone to point north, then east. Ask: *"If I am facing north and turn $90^\circ$ clockwise, which way am I facing?"* Answer: East.

### 3. Quick Compass Challenge (5 mins)
Everyone starts in the middle. Call a direction or simple clue and Scouts move/point to the correct wall. Examples: "East", "180 degrees", "opposite west", "90 degrees clockwise from south". Keep it fast.

---

### 4. Orienteering Bingo (30 mins)

#### Recommended Format
1. Group scouts into 2s or 3s and give each one board and a pen/counters.
2. Draw one calling card at a time. Keep drawn cards separate so you can check a winning board.
3. Say the word on the card or use the clues.
4. Award a small prize for the first completed horizontal, vertical or diagonal lines.
5. Continue to a full house.
6. Can always print more board and rest for new rounds if the scouts are enjoying it.

#### Reinforcement
When somebody calls "Bingo!", ask them to explain one or two of the marked terms before confirming the win. It reinforces learning without turning the whole game into a test.

### 5. Indoor Orienteering-Based Games (25 mins)
*Don't force these - these are just a bit of reinforcement with fun. If scouts are done with Orienteering, expand on section 6.*

- **Compass Corners (5-10 mins, almost no setup):** Everyone starts in the middle. Call a direction or clue and Scouts move to the correct wall. Examples: "East", "180°", "opposite east", "90° clockwise from south". Add NE/SE/SW/NW once they are confident.
- **Human Compass (5 mins, no equipment):** Everyone faces north. Call instructions such as "$90^\circ$ clockwise", "180°", or "$45^\circ$ anticlockwise". Scouts physically turn to face the answer. Wrong direction = one strike/point rather than sitting out.
- **Compass Simon Says (5-10 mins, no equipment):** Play Simon Says using navigation instructions: "Simon says face north"; "Simon says turn $90^\circ$ clockwise"; "Simon says point south-west". Anyone who moves when Simon did not say, or chooses the wrong direction, gets a strike.
- **Map Symbol Charades (5-10 mins, use the bingo cards):** A Scout draws a calling card and mimes the term without speaking. Their Patrol/team gets 30 seconds to identify it. Good cards include river/stream, woodland, steep slope, building, footpath and orienteering control.
- **Blind Navigation (10 mins, a few chairs/cones):** Create a very simple obstacle route. One Scout closes their eyes while a partner guides them using only compass directions and numbers of steps: "three steps north", "two east", etc. No left/right/towards me. Swap roles.
