# Navigator Staged Activity Badge, Stage 3

**Badge:** [Scouts Navigator Staged Activity Badge, Stage 3](https://www.scouts.org.uk/staged-badges/navigator/stage-3/) — mostly passive, with one hall session covering the map-reading knowledge.

**Why this works as a filler:** the badge's real navigating requirement — walking a route of at least 5km, navigating for at least part of the journey — is already covered by the group's standing Night Hikes and any optional hikes counted toward Hikes Away, with no dedicated badge term needed. This session covers the map-reading knowledge and kit awareness that can be taught indoors, so Scouts arrive at those hikes able to actually navigate rather than just follow along.

**Requirements this session covers:**

> "Learn how to read a six figure grid reference."

> "Understand contour lines on an Ordnance Survey map."

> "Using 1:50000 and 1:25000 scale Ordnance Survey maps show that you understand the meaning of scale. Can set the map to north and can recognise conventional map symbols."

> "Show you know how to dress appropriately and what kit you and your group will need."

**Requirements covered passively elsewhere:**

> "Follow and walk a route of at least 5km, using a map to navigate for at least part of the journey. Your Leader can plan the route but you'll work with your team, or take turns, to navigate." — covered whenever a Scout takes a genuine navigating turn on one of the group's standing Night Hikes, or an optional hike counted toward Hikes Away.

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Tally check (10 min)

Quick round: has each Scout had a genuine navigating turn on a Night Hike or optional hike yet, or is one coming up? Best run before an upcoming hike so Scouts arrive able to use what they've just learned.

### Grid references & symbols (30 min)

Using real OS maps (1:50000 and 1:25000), practise reading and giving six-figure grid references, setting a map to north using a compass or natural features, and recognising conventional map symbols. Small-group map-reading races or scavenger-hunt-style grid-reference challenges work well here.

### Contours & scale (40 min)

Using the same maps, explain what contour lines represent and how to read them, and make sure every Scout understands the meaning of map scale (why 1:25000 shows more detail over less ground than 1:50000). A relief model, a 3D contour puzzle, or simply comparing a steep local hill's contours against a flat area works well as a visual aid.

### Dress & kit (20 min)

Discuss what to wear and pack for a navigating hike depending on weather and terrain — layers, waterproofs, appropriate footwear, a map case, spare compass batteries if using anything electronic, food and water. Tie this directly to whatever hike is coming up next so it's concrete rather than abstract.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Leaders should note which Scouts have completed this session, and separately track each Scout's actual navigated 5km walk (e.g. in Online Scout Manager) — this repo doesn't track individual Scout progress.
- Whoever plans the next Night Hike after this session should make sure at least one Scout per patrol gets a genuine navigating turn, not just tag along.
