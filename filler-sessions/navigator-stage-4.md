# Navigator Staged Activity Badge, Stage 4

**Badge:** [Scouts Navigator Staged Activity Badge, Stage 4](https://www.scouts.org.uk/staged-badges/navigator/stage-4/) — mostly passive, with one hall session covering the compass/map knowledge. Builds on [Stage 3](navigator-stage-3.md).

**Why this works as a filler:** the badge's real navigating requirement — walking two compass routes of at least 5km each — is already covered by the group's standing Night Hikes and any optional hikes counted toward Hikes Away, with no dedicated badge term needed. This session covers the bearings, advanced map-interpretation, and kit-choice knowledge that can be taught indoors (with a short spell in the car park or a nearby field for the physical bearing-walking technique), so Scouts arrive at those hikes able to navigate independently rather than under close supervision.

**Requirements this session covers:**

> "Show you know how to: convert grid bearings to magnetic bearings and vice versa; use back bearings to check the route; estimate your current position using a compass; walk on a bearing (including 'deviating from course' — the four right angles technique to bypass an obstacle, also known as 'boxing'); read a six figure grid reference."

> "Using 1:50000 and 1:25000 scale Ordnance Survey maps: interpret contour lines in terms of shape and steepness of terrain, and learn what the topographical features mean, including valley, ridge, spur, col; show how to set a map, with and without a compass; learn how to use and give six-figure grid references, and demonstrate the use of a roamer to improve accuracy."

> "Show you know how to dress appropriately for the walk and what kit you and your group need."

> "Choose the most appropriate type of map for the journey you are taking."

**Requirements covered passively elsewhere:**

> "Walk two compass routes of at least 5 kilometres each. They should be defined on a map, one route's start and end points defined by you and the other by an adult." — covered whenever a Scout navigates independently on two of the group's standing Night Hikes, or optional hikes counted toward Hikes Away.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Tally check |
| 0:20–1:00 | 40 min | Bearings & boxing (indoor theory + outdoor practice) |
| 1:00–1:40 | 40 min | Advanced map reading |
| 1:40–1:55 | 15 min | Kit & map choice |
| 1:55–2:00 | 5 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Tally check (10 min)

Quick round: has each Scout completed Stage 3 already, and have they had a fully independent (rather than shared/team) navigating turn yet? This stage assumes Stage 3's grid-reference and map-symbol basics are already solid.

### Bearings & boxing (40 min)

Indoors: teach converting grid bearings to magnetic bearings and back, taking and using back bearings, and estimating position from a compass. Outdoors (car park, field, or hall grounds): practise walking on a bearing, including the "boxing" technique for going around an obstacle using four right-angle turns. This is the one part of the session that genuinely needs some outdoor space, even if not a full hike.

### Advanced map reading (40 min)

Using real OS maps, interpret contour shapes for valleys, ridges, spurs, and cols; practise setting a map with and without a compass; and use a roamer to give more accurate six-figure grid references than Stage 3's approximate method.

### Kit & map choice (15 min)

Building on Stage 3's kit discussion, cover choosing the most appropriate map (scale, area coverage, waterproofing) for a specific type of journey.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Leaders should note which Scouts have completed this session, and separately track each Scout's two independent 5km compass routes (e.g. in Online Scout Manager) — this repo doesn't track individual Scout progress.
- Whoever plans the next two Night Hikes after this session should make sure each Scout going for Stage 4 gets a genuinely independent navigating turn, defined on a map by themselves for one route and by an adult for the other, not a shared/team turn like Stage 3.
