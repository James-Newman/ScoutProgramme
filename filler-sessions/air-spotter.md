# Air Spotter Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Air Spotter Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/air-spotter/)

**Why it's a good filler:** the badge explicitly offers "taking photographs **or collecting pictures**" as an alternative to visiting an airfield — collecting pictures from books, magazines, or the internet needs no venue and no external logistics at all.

**Adaptation needed:** none for the requirements themselves — but this session depends on a leader gathering a picture pack in advance (see Materials). That's normal preparation, not an adapted requirement.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> "taking photographs or collecting pictures of at least 10 different aircraft types and naming them with their uses"

Plus one of the following (this plan uses national markings):

> "recognise and name national aircraft markings of at least six countries including the UK"

Plus:

> "describe the main features used to recognise six aircraft chosen by an appropriate adult"

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Briefing |
| 0:20–0:55 | 35 min | Collect and name 10 aircraft types |
| 0:55–1:20 | 25 min | National markings |
| 1:20–1:50 | 30 min | Describe six aircraft |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Briefing (10 min)

Explain the three tasks and how they build on each other — the picture pack from the first task gets reused for the third.

### Collect and name 10 aircraft types (35 min)

Each Scout works through the pre-gathered picture pack (see Materials), picking out at least 10 different aircraft types, naming each, and noting its use (passenger airliner, cargo, military fighter, trainer, helicopter, glider, and so on). Working in pairs is fine if pictures are limited.

### National markings (25 min)

Using the reference sheet (see Materials), Scouts learn to recognise and name the national aircraft markings (roundels/flashes) of at least six countries including the UK. Run it as a quickfire matching game: hold up a marking, first correct answer gets a point.

### Describe six aircraft (30 min)

A leader picks six aircraft from the picture pack in advance. Each Scout describes the main features used to recognise each one (wing shape, engine count and position, tail shape, size, distinguishing markings). Pairs or small groups work well here, comparing notes before reporting back.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Materials

- **A picture pack**, gathered by a leader beforehand: at least 15–20 clear pictures of different aircraft types (passenger jets, propeller aircraft, helicopters, military aircraft, gliders), printed from a public source (aviation enthusiast sites, Wikipedia, or a library book) or brought in as magazine cuttings. This is the one piece of prep this session depends on — build the pack once and it's reusable every time this filler session runs.
- **A national markings reference sheet** — printable roundel charts are widely available from aviation reference sites and Wikipedia's "military aircraft insignia" pages; a leader should print one covering at least six countries including the UK.
- Pens and paper for naming/describing.

## Notes

- This session can be run entirely from printed materials with no aircraft, airfield, or specialist knowledge needed on the day — the leader's only job beforehand is assembling the picture pack.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities.
