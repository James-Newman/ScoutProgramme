# World Faiths Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts World Faiths Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/world-faiths/)

**Why it's a good filler:** two of the badge's three parts are straightforwardly single-session (learning about a faith's founder, reading and discussing a text); it's the third part that needs adapting to fit a standalone hall session.

**Adaptation needed:** light. Part 1 requires visiting a place of worship or attending a festival — neither fits a single session on demand. This plan substitutes a video tour of, or a guest speaker from, a faith unfamiliar to the group, which is a like-for-like substitute for the experience the requirement is after (learning what a place of worship for that faith is actually like), not a skipped requirement. If your group can arrange a real visit instead, that's the badge exactly as written — treat the substitution below as the fallback, not the ideal.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> Part 1: "Visit a place of worship for a faith that you are not familiar with and find out the differences between this building and another place of worship" **(adapted this session to a video tour + Q&A — see Session detail)**, or "Attend a festival or event linked to a faith that you are not familiar with."
>
> Part 2: "Learn about the life of a founder or a prominent leader of a faith (such as Prince Siddhartha Gautama, Mohammed, Jesus Christ or a saint like St George)," or "Find out about someone whose faith has had a significant impact upon their life."
>
> Part 3: "Read a text from a faith that you are not familiar with and show or discuss how this compares to your own beliefs."

| Time | Duration | Block |

| 0:10–0:40 | 30 min | Video tour or guest speaker, plus Q&A |
| 0:40–1:10 | 30 min | Founder or influential person |
| 1:10–1:40 | 30 min | Read and discuss a text |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Reflection |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening. Frame the session honestly: this is exploring a faith most of the group probably isn't familiar with, and differences of belief in the room are welcome, not something to gloss over.

### Video tour or guest speaker, plus Q&A (30 min)

Pick a faith genuinely unfamiliar to most of the group. If a guest speaker from that faith community can be arranged in advance, that's the stronger option and gets closer to the spirit of an actual visit. Otherwise, a good-quality video tour of a place of worship for that faith (many faith organisations publish these) followed by group discussion of what's different about the building compared with a place of worship the group is more familiar with — layout, symbols, what happens there, how people behave inside.

**Materials:** a device to show video, or a confirmed guest speaker.

### Founder or influential person (30 min)

In small groups, research the life of a founder or prominent leader of a faith (the badge's own examples: Siddhartha Gautama, Muhammad, Jesus Christ, or a saint), or alternatively research someone whose faith has significantly shaped their life. Each group shares a short summary with the room.

**Materials:** internet access or reference books.

### Read and discuss a text (30 min)

Provide a short excerpt from a text of the faith being studied. **Source this thoughtfully** — ideally with input from a faith advisor, a member of that faith community, or an established interfaith education resource, rather than picking an excerpt without that context. Scouts read the excerpt individually or in pairs, then discuss as a group how it compares with their own beliefs (or lack of them) — there's no right answer here, the requirement is the comparison and discussion, not agreement.

**Materials:** printed text excerpt (sourced as above).

### Reflection (10 min)

Quick round: one thing that surprised them, one similarity they noticed between this faith and their own beliefs (or another faith they know), one difference.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- This is the one filler session that depends on getting the content right, not just the logistics — take care with how the faith being studied is represented, and where possible involve someone from that faith community in choosing the video/text rather than relying on a leader's own research alone.
- If you have been confirmed, had a bar/bat mitzvah, or a similar faith-life milestone yourself, the Scout Association's own guidance allows the equivalent "My Faith" badge to be satisfied by taking part in a Scouting activity relating to your own faith instead — that's a different badge to this one, but worth knowing if it comes up.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities.
