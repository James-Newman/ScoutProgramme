# World Faiths Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts World Faiths Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/world-faiths/)

**Why it's a good filler:** needs no theme and no earlier sessions, and the research is ready-made. Each patrol gets a printed pack on one faith, so there's no internet research to supervise. The faith-specific content is on paper, and the session ends with 45 minutes of games.

**Adaptation needed:** light to moderate. Part 1 asks for a visit to a place of worship or a festival, which doesn't fit a single hall session. This plan substitutes a short video and each patrol's research into where its faith worships. That's a reasonable adaptation rather than the requirement as written; if your group can arrange a real visit, that's the badge exactly as written. The plan is run as written; [Badge coverage](#badge-coverage) records how each requirement is met, including the text-reading requirement, which the plan doesn't include a reading step for.

**Requirements this session covers** (live wording, checked October 2026):

> Complete one of these activities (Part 1):
>
> - Visit a place of worship for a faith that you are not familiar with. Find out the differences between this building and another place of worship.
> - Attend a festival or event linked to a faith that you are not familiar with.
>
> Complete one of these activities (Part two):
>
> - Learn about the life of a founder or a prominent leader of a faith. You could learn about people like Prince Siddhartha Gautama, Mohammed, Jesus Christ or a saint such as St George.
> - Find out about someone whose faith has had a significant impact upon their life.
>
> Read a text from a faith that you are not familiar with. Show or discuss how this compares to your own beliefs.
>
> Find out how following the teachings of a particular faith affects an individual's daily life. This could include food or dietary laws, rituals, prayers and worship, or religious observances.

## Session detail

### Welcome, notices & flag (10 min)

Notices and opening flag. Sit the Scouts in four patrol groups, one per faith:

- Christianity
- Islam
- Hinduism
- Sikhism

The session is built around four patrols. With fewer than four, drop a faith (the [Hinduism](../resources/world-faiths-patrol-packs.md#research-pack-hinduism) pack is the only one with no matching video, so it's the easiest to lose). With more than four, split a faith between two patrols; with the troop's maximum of 6 patrols, two faiths will be doubled up.

**Materials:** tables and chairs for four patrols.

### Intro video & discussion (8 min)

**Leaders: pick one video only.** All three are from the BBC.

- Christianity: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yEtR2ouw76c>
- Islam: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c5c9-1zxPeA>
- Sikhism: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qXhV3dMxSzE>

Watch it as a whole troop, then discuss:

1. What did you learn?
2. What surprised you?
3. What values did you hear about?
4. Were any of those values similar to Scout Values?

**Materials:** projector and laptop.

### Patrol investigation challenge (25 min)

Each patrol receives one [research pack](../resources/world-faiths-patrol-packs.md) and a patrol recording sheet. Their task is to discover:

1. Where did this faith begin?
2. Who founded it (if applicable)?
3. What is the holy book?
4. Where do followers worship?
5. Name an important festival.
6. How does the faith affect everyday life?
7. What values seem important in this faith?
8. Which Scout Values connect most strongly?
9. What surprised your patrol most?

They record their answers on the [patrol recording sheet](../resources/world-faiths-patrol-packs.md#patrol-recording-sheet), then use A4/A3 paper to prepare a 2–3 minute presentation.

**Materials:** one research pack per patrol, one recording sheet per patrol, pens and pencils, A4/A3 paper for presentation notes.

### Patrol presentations (12 min)

Each patrol gives its 2–3 minute presentation. After each one, ask the room:

- Did anybody learn something new?
- Which Scout Value did you hear most strongly?
- What similarities did you notice?

Draw out these values as they come up:

- Respect
- Honesty
- Service
- Compassion
- Community
- Equality

**Materials:** none.

### Games (45 min)

A 45-minute games block. The plan doesn't specify the games, so choose them to suit the group.

**Materials:** whatever the chosen games need.

### Reflection (10 min)

Discuss as a troop:

- What surprised you most tonight?
- Which faith sounded most familiar?
- Which faith sounded least familiar?
- Did any values appear in more than one faith?
- What values did all four groups have in common?
- Why is it important to understand people who believe different things?
- How can Scouts show respect to people from different backgrounds?

**Key message:** although people may have different beliefs, many communities share values such as:

- Kindness
- Honesty
- Respect
- Helping others
- Service
- Community

These ideas connect strongly with Scout Values.

**Materials:** none.

### Closing flag & notices (10 min)

Notices and closing flag.

**Thought for the week:** "You don't have to share someone's beliefs to learn from them, respect them, or be their friend."

**Materials:** none.

## Badge coverage

Checked against the live requirement wording above, for the plan as written.

| Requirement | Where | Status |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Part 1: place of worship, and how it differs from another | The video, and investigation question 4 ("Where do followers worship?") across four different faiths | **Adapted.** The packs describe each building, but no question asks the patrol to compare it with another. Prompt for that comparison in [Patrol presentations](#patrol-presentations-12-min). Also check the video you choose actually shows inside a place of worship; the links haven't been verified against this repo. |
| Part 2: a founder or prominent leader | Investigation question 2, and the pack for each faith | **Met, lightly.** Christianity, Islam and Sikhism each have a named founder in the pack. Hinduism has none, so that patrol should name a prominent figure, or not claim this part. |
| Read a text and compare with own beliefs | Investigation question 3 only names the holy book | **Not covered by the plan as written.** Scouts never read an excerpt from a holy text, or compare it with their own beliefs. If a leader wants it covered, a short excerpt from one of the faiths, sourced with the same care as the packs (ideally with input from someone from that faith community), read in patrols and compared with the Scouts' own beliefs, would close it; otherwise note it as outstanding for any Scout claiming the badge. |
| Daily life of someone following the faith | Investigation question 6, and the daily-life paragraph in each pack | **Met.** |

## Notes

- This is the one filler session that depends on getting the content right, not just the logistics. Take care with how each faith is represented and, where possible, ask someone from that faith community to look over the video and packs.
- The packs are in the repo as [printable resources](../resources/world-faiths-patrol-packs.md); no advance sourcing is needed beyond picking the video and checking it plays.
- The three video links are YouTube; check they still play and aren't blocked on the hall's connection before the session.
- If you have been confirmed, had a bar/bat mitzvah, or a similar faith-life milestone yourself, the Scout Association's own guidance allows the equivalent "My Faith" badge to be satisfied by taking part in a Scouting activity relating to your own faith instead; that's a different badge to this one, but worth knowing if it comes up.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities.
