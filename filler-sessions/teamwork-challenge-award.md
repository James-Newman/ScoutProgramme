# Teamwork Challenge Award

**Badge:** [Scouts Teamwork Challenge Award](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/awards/teamwork/) — mostly passive, with one reflection session. Worked towards "over a three-month period."

**Why this works as a filler:** three of the four requirements accumulate automatically via things every term already does — regular patrol activities count as "being part of a Scout team," a varied icebreaker in each term's Welcome Session covers new team-building activities, and Welcome Session's "Scouts agree their own rules" block already functions as a Patrol/Troop Forum. This session delivers the one reflective requirement that needs real facilitation.

**Requirements this session covers:**

> "Give at least three examples of when you've been in different types of teams. Explain your role in those teams."

**Requirements covered passively elsewhere (see `AGENTS.md` for the standing conventions):**

> "On at least three separate occasions, be part of a Scout team, where you work together to achieve a goal." — regular patrol activities, camps, and patrol-led sessions.

> "Take part in at least three team building activities that you have not tried before." — Welcome Session's icebreaker, provided it's genuinely varied term to term.

> "Take an active part in at least four Troop or Patrol Forums. At each forum, express your views on at least one item being discussed." — Welcome Session's "Scouts agree their own rules" block, counted once per term.

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Tally check |
| 0:20–1:40 | 80 min | Three teams, three roles |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Share |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Tally check (10 min)

Quick round per Scout: how many team occasions, new team-building activities, and Patrol/Troop Forums can they already point to from this term and previous ones? This is mostly a leader bookkeeping exercise, done out loud so Scouts get a sense of their own progress too.

### Three teams, three roles (80 min)

One-to-one or in small groups, each Scout works through three genuine examples of teams they've been part of — these don't have to be Scouts specifically (a school sports team, a class project group, a family task) as long as at least the required occasions come from Scouting. For each, they explain: what was the team, what was the goal, and what was *their* role in it (leader, quiet contributor, the one who kept everyone on schedule, whatever's true). Write these up properly — a sentence or two each is enough, but vague ("I was just in the team") isn't. This is a good one to spread across the room with a leader circulating rather than one leader doing all three examples for every Scout in sequence.

### Share (10 min)

A few Scouts share one of their three examples with the room — not compulsory, but usually someone has a good story.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- Leaders should note each Scout's three examples and roles as the evidence for the reflective requirement.
- The three passively-covered requirements (team occasions, new team-building activities, forums) are best tracked centrally (e.g. Online Scout Manager) across terms — this repo doesn't track individual Scout progress, only where the opportunity comes from.
- If a Scout is short on genuinely varied team-building activities because Welcome Session icebreakers have repeated too often, that's worth flagging back to whoever's planning Welcome Sessions rather than trying to fix it in this session.
