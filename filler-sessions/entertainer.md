# Entertainer Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Entertainer Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/entertainer/)

**Why it's a good filler:** Option 1 of the badge explicitly allows presenting "for your Troop" as the audience — not a school, not a parents' evening, just the room you're already in. That makes the whole badge completable in a single sitting with zero external logistics.

**Adaptation needed:** none. "For your Troop" is one of the badge's own three listed audience options, not a substitution.

**Requirements completed this session (Option 1):**

> "Write and plan some original entertainment involving a campfire or stage routine involving mime, drama, music, storytelling or conjuring. Alternatively, it could take the form of a presentation featuring sketches, film, slides, tapes, photography or sound recordings."
>
> "Rehearse the entertainment and make sure everyone has a job to do. You might need actors, a producer, a stage manager, front of house or a publicity manager."
>
> "Present the entertainment to an audience at a school, for your Troop or at a parents' evening."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Brief & roles |
| 0:20–0:50 | 30 min | Write and plan |
| 0:50–1:20 | 30 min | Rehearse |
| 1:20–1:40 | 20 min | Perform |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Debrief |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Brief & roles (10 min)

Split into patrols (or smaller groups if patrols are large). Explain the badge needs "everyone has a job to do" — not everyone has to perform. Introduce the role list: actor, producer, stage manager, front of house, publicity manager, and let groups divvy up who does what before they start planning content.

### Write and plan (30 min)

Each group writes and plans a short piece of original entertainment — the badge's own list of formats is wide: mime, drama, music, storytelling, conjuring, or a sketch/film/slides/photography presentation. Keep the target short (3–5 minutes performed) so six groups can all get through it in the time available.

### Rehearse (30 min)

Run through the piece at least twice. The stage manager and producer roles should be actively directing, not just watching — that's the point of assigning them.

### Perform (20 min)

Each group presents to the rest of the Troop, who are the audience for this — that's one of the badge's own sanctioned options, not a workaround. Keep it moving; a compere (a Scout, not a leader) introducing each act keeps the energy up between performances.

### Debrief (10 min)

Quick round: one thing that went well, one thing they'd do differently. This also confirms every Scout actually had a job and took part, which is what gets signed off.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Materials

Whatever each group's chosen format needs — costume/prop box if available, a phone or camera if a group goes the film/photography route, nothing at all if they go for mime or storytelling.

## Notes

- This works as well as a genuine drop-in filler as it does a planned session — groups can be as ambitious or as simple as the time allows.
- If a group's piece runs long in rehearsal, trim it rather than extend the performance block — five short pieces are better than three that overrun.
