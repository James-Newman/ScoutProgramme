# Campfire Foil Parcels

**Badge:** None — a standalone fun session, not a badge in its own right. It does give real evidence towards the Outdoor Challenge Award, though (see [Notes](#notes)).

**Why it's a good filler:** needs no theme and no earlier sessions. Scouts light their own fire, prepare their own food and cook it in the embers, so the whole meeting is hands-on. A cheap shopping list, easy to scale for any number of Scouts, and a hot meal at the end of it.

**Adaptation needed:** None, provided the meeting place has an outdoor space where fires are allowed (altar fires are the easiest way to do this on a hall's grounds).

Tested with 16 Scouts in four groups of four. The recipe, in a form each group can use on the night, is the [Campfire Foil Parcel recipe card](../resources/campfire-foil-parcel-recipe-card.md).

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome & safety briefing |
| 0:10–0:35 | 25 min | Light fires, prepare food |
| 0:35–0:50 | 15 min | Build and seal parcels |
| 0:50–1:15 | 25 min | Cook in the embers |
| 1:15–1:30 | 15 min | Check, rest and eat |
| 1:30–1:50 | 20 min | Put the fires out and clear up |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

The whole session hangs on the fires being down to embers by 0:50, so the fire-lighting block is the one not to let slip.

## Session detail

### Welcome & safety briefing (10 min)

Notices, patrol flags, opening. Split into groups of four (they worked well; every Scout has a job). Then a quick, firm safety briefing before anyone goes near a fire:

- Fires are lit only at the altar fires, only in the fire-lighting block, and only with a leader in sight.
- Water bucket and sand bucket beside every fire, in reach, from the moment it's lit.
- Tongs and heat-proof gloves for anything on or near the fire; nothing is ever lifted by hand.
- Walk around the fire, never run; long hair tied back, loose sleeves and hoods tucked in.
- Hands washed before touching food, and again after raw sausages or bacon; raw meat gets its own board and utensils.
- Parcels are opened by a leader, away from faces, because the steam is hot enough to burn.

**Materials:** none.

### Light fires, prepare food (25 min)

Split each group in two. **Two Scouts go to the altar fire and light it; the other two start food prep.**

- **Fire-lighters:** use the fire kit already set out at the altar fire. Build up slowly with kindling, then fuel, and keep the fire fed so it burns down evenly. The fire should end up as glowing orange embers with no big flames before anything goes on it.
- **Food-preppers:** wash hands, scrub and cube the potatoes into 1.5 cm pieces, slice the onions into thin wedges, and toss both with the oil/butter, salt, pepper and herbs in a bowl. Keep raw sausages and bacon on their own board and wash hands again after handling them.

Leaders move between groups; the knife work and the fire are the two things that need an adult watching. Keep the cubes small and even, since potato size decides whether the parcels cook through.

**Materials:** altar fires (one per group), fuel and kindling, fire kit, water and sand buckets, chopping boards, knives, bowls, ingredients, hand-washing kit.

### Build and seal parcels (15 min)

The whole group comes together at its table, with one Scout at a time on fire-watch (see the cook block below). Each Scout builds their own parcel, following the [recipe card](../resources/campfire-foil-parcel-recipe-card.md): two layers of foil, a little oil in the middle, veg mix, 2 sausages, 2 rashers of bacon over the top, then fold the long edges over twice and crimp both ends so it's fully sealed with a little air space inside.

Each Scout labels their parcel with their name (marker pen on the foil, or a twist of foil), because everything looks identical once it's been in the fire.

**Materials:** heavy-duty foil (2 sheets of about 40 cm square per Scout), oil for the foil, marker pens.

### Cook in the embers (25 min)

Check the fire is at the embers stage before any parcel goes on; if it still has big flames, wait. Then:

1. A leader checks each fire, then Scouts use tongs to place their parcels on the hot embers, not in flames.
2. Turn each parcel once, halfway through, with the tongs.
3. Parcels need **20–25 minutes** on good embers. Allow up to 35 if the embers have started to fade.

Rotate the Scouts on fire-watch every 5 minutes or so, so each of the four takes a turn at tending the fire and handling the tongs. The rest of the group can run a low-setup game nearby or learn a camp song for next time; keep it within sight of the fire.

**Materials:** long tongs and heat-proof gloves per fire.

### Check, rest and eat (15 min)

A leader lifts each parcel out with tongs and lets it rest for 2 minutes, then opens it away from faces. Check before anyone eats: the potatoes should be soft when pierced with a fork, and the sausages cooked all the way through, with no pink inside and the juices running clear (75 °C at the centre, if you have a probe thermometer). If not, reseal and return to the embers for 5–10 minutes.

Let the parcels cool for a few minutes more, then eat straight from the foil, taking care with hot steam and fat.

**Materials:** forks, probe thermometer (optional), a few spare plates for anyone who wants one.

### Put the fires out and clear up (20 min)

Used foil goes in the bin, not the fire and not the ground. Put out each fire properly (a leader's job, following the group's own method for the altar fires), then check by hand-hovering above the embers, not by touching, that there's no heat left. Wash up the boards, bowls and utensils, wipe down the tables, and leave the space as you found it.

**Materials:** bin bags, washing-up kit, water.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, flag down, home.

## Shopping list

Portions per Scout: ½–1 potato, ¼ onion, 2 sausages, 2 rashers of streaky bacon, and 2 sheets of foil.

| Item | Per group of 4 | 16 Scouts | 24 Scouts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pork sausages | 8 | 32 | 48 |
| Streaky bacon rashers | 8 | 32 | 48 |
| Medium potatoes | 2–4 | 8–16 | 12–24 |
| Onions | 1 | 4 | 6 |
| Vegetable oil or butter | 3 tbsp (2 for the veg, 1 for the foil) | 12 tbsp | 18 tbsp |
| Salt | 1 tsp | 4 tsp | 6 tsp |
| Black pepper | ½ tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp |
| Mixed dried herbs (optional) | 1 tsp | 4 tsp | 6 tsp |
| Heavy-duty foil, ~40 cm sheets | 8 | 32 | 48 |

Buy potatoes at the top of the range if the group is hungry. For a veggie group, swap in veggie sausages and skip the bacon, and use vegetable oil rather than butter if anyone is vegan. If pork is an issue for anyone, buy pork-free sausages for them. For extra flavour, a knob of butter, some grated cheese or a splash of ketchup or BBQ sauce can go in before sealing.

## Other materials

- Altar fires, one per group, with fuel, kindling and fire kit (matches/firesteels, firelighters), set up before the Scouts arrive.
- A water bucket and a sand bucket per fire, plus a first aid kit with burns provision, and water for cooling burns.
- Long tongs and heat-proof gloves for each fire.
- Chopping boards, knives, bowls and utensils for the veg, and separate boards and utensils for the raw meat.
- Hand-washing station or sanitiser, with soap and water, near the prep tables.
- Marker pens, bin bags, washing-up kit.
- Head torches or lighting for the prep tables, if it will be dark.

## Notes

- Set the altar fires and the fire kit up in advance; it saves the time lost at the start of the fire-lighting block, which is the one that decides whether the embers are ready.
- Needs an outdoor space where fires are allowed, a completed fire risk assessment per group policy, and a knife-use risk assessment covering ratios and supervision for the potato cutting. Check the group's own policy on leaders per fire.
- Check allergies and dietary needs with parents before the session (gluten in some sausages is the usual one), and have a veggie option ready.
- Bring a wet-weather decision: if the weather rules out fires, swap to another filler session rather than trying to cook indoors.
- If the wood is wet, or you're short of time, a leader can pre-light the fires about 20 minutes early. Scouts still tend the fires and cook, but they'll have missed the lighting itself.
- **Outdoor Challenge Award evidence:** this session delivers requirement 4, "Prepare and light an open fire or set up a suitable stove. Use it to prepare, cook and serve a meal safely", and the optional extra "Cook a backwoods meal with the group". Leaders should note who lit a fire, who prepared food, who tended the fire, and who served safely. The rotation in the cook block exists so every Scout has a turn on the fire. It does not cover knife safety (requirement 10), which still needs [Fire Safety & Camp Cooking Prep](outdoor-challenge-award/fire-safety-and-camp-cooking.md), and it can't count towards Chef Activity Badge, which needs a planned two-course meal.
