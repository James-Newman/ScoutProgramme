# Media Relations & Marketing Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Media Relations & Marketing Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/media-relations-marketing/) (the current name for what used to be called "Public Relations")

**Why it's a good filler:** the badge is "choose 3 of these [seven]" — three of the seven need nothing beyond the room you're in: research, a presentation to your own group, and a display. No press release to actually send, no real event to interview someone about.

**Adaptation needed:** none. All three chosen tasks are picked directly from the badge's own list.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> "Produce and give a presentation about Scouting, using audio and visual media and thinking about how to make it relevant to people not involved in Scouting"
>
> "Find out about local media outlets such as radio, TV, newspapers and online, and work out a storyline that they might be interested in featuring"
>
> "Prepare a creative display about your Troop or Group that can be exhibited in your local library, information centre or public place"

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Task briefing |
| 0:20–0:50 | 30 min | Local media research |
| 0:50–1:25 | 35 min | Creative display |
| 1:25–1:50 | 25 min | Presentation prep & delivery |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Task briefing (10 min)

Split into patrols. Each patrol works on all three tasks over the session, but assign a lead role per task within the patrol (a "researcher," a "designer," a "presenter") so nothing gets neglected in the group work.

### Local media research (30 min)

Each patrol researches local media outlets — local paper, local radio, community Facebook groups/websites — and works out one storyline about the Troop that outlet might realistically feature (a badge achievement, a camp, a community project). No need to actually contact anyone; the requirement is finding out and working out a storyline, not placing the story.

**Materials:** internet access (phones/tablets/laptops), or pre-printed information about local outlets if the group doesn't have internet access at the meeting place.

### Creative display (35 min)

Each patrol designs a display about the Troop or Group suitable for exhibition somewhere public (a library, community centre notice board) — photos, artwork, a written blurb, whatever format the patrol chooses. It doesn't need to actually be exhibited to complete the requirement ("prepare a creative display... that can be exhibited"), though putting a good one up somewhere real afterwards is a nice bonus, not part of this session's scope.

**Materials:** poster paper/card, pens, glue, printed photos if available, scissors.

### Presentation prep & delivery (25 min)

Each patrol puts together a short presentation about Scouting aimed at people who've never been involved — 15 minutes to prepare (slides, a poster, or just well-organised talking points plus something visual), then each patrol presents for 2–3 minutes to the rest of the room, who play the "people not involved in Scouting" audience.

**Materials:** whatever the patrol chooses for visual media — a tablet/laptop for slides, or a hand-drawn poster works just as well.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Notes

- All three tasks run in parallel within each patrol rather than as a whole-room sequence, so a patrol that finishes research early can start the display sooner — keep a rough eye on timing rather than enforcing a hard stop-start for every block.
- Leaders should note engagement across the three tasks per patrol as the evidence for sign-off; there's no single "big finish" moment like a performance, so it's worth checking in with each patrol at least once per block.
