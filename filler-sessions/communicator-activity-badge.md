# Communicator Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Communicator Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/communicator/) — Option 3, Mobile and Internet Communication.

**Why it's a good filler:** the badge offers 5 options, and this is the only one that needs no external equipment, licence, guest speaker, or ongoing multi-week practice — Radio Communication needs a licence or station visit, Communication Codes needs sustained practice speed plus building an oscillator, Foreign Languages needs a fluent conversation partner and ongoing exposure, and Translator needs a braille/BSL-fluent guest. Mobile and Internet Communication's 8 tasks are all doable in one sitting using Scouts' own phones/devices (or a leader's, if a Scout doesn't have one).

**Adaptation needed:** light — task 7 (sharing photos/videos of a past Scouting activity) needs existing media to work with. Ask Scouts to bring photos/videos already on their phones from a recent camp or activity, or have a leader gather a small shared pack in advance as a fallback for anyone who doesn't have any.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> "Show you know how to use your mobile safely and how to keep it safe."

> "Learn the meaning of these terms: SMS, MMS, 3G, 4G, WAP and Bluetooth."

> "Send a creative text, multimedia or video message to invite a friend to a Scouts event."

> "Manage a mobile phone address book and set up groups of contacts."

> "Show you can text accurately at a rate of 50 characters per minute."

> "Show you know the meaning of some popular chat abbreviations."

> "Share photos and videos of a Scouting activity you've been involved with, using available technology."

> "Manage an email address book and set up groups of contacts."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |

| 0:10–0:30 | 20 min | Mobile safety & terminology |
| 0:30–0:55 | 25 min | Contacts & inboxes |
| 0:55–1:20 | 25 min | Texting speed & abbreviations |
| 1:20–1:45 | 25 min | Creative message & photo/video share |
| 1:45–1:55 | 10 min | Share |
| 1:55–2:00 | 5 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Mobile safety & terminology (20 min)

Discuss keeping a mobile phone physically safe (not left unattended, screen lock, reporting loss/theft) and safe to use (privacy settings, not sharing personal details or location with strangers, what to do about unwanted messages). Then quickfire through the six terms — SMS, MMS, 3G, 4G, WAP, Bluetooth — Scouts explain each in their own words before a leader confirms.

### Contacts & inboxes (25 min)

Each Scout sets up or reviews a group of contacts in their phone's address book (e.g. "Scouts — [Patrol name]"), and does the same for an email address book if they have one — a shared leader email account works for anyone who doesn't.

### Texting speed & abbreviations (25 min)

Time each Scout typing a set passage to check they can manage at least 50 characters per minute. Follow with a quickfire round matching popular chat abbreviations (LOL, BRB, OMW, and so on) to their meanings.

### Creative message & photo/video share (25 min)

Each Scout composes and sends a creative text, multimedia, or video message inviting a friend to a real or made-up Scouts event, then shares a photo or video from a past Scouting activity (their own, or from the leader's fallback pack) using whatever technology is available (AirDrop, messaging app, group chat).

### Share (10 min)

A few Scouts show their creative invite message or shared photo/video to the room.

### Close (5 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Materials

- A small fallback pack of photos/videos from a recent Scouting activity, gathered by a leader beforehand, for any Scout without their own to share.
- A timed passage of text (roughly 50+ characters) for the texting-speed check, and a stopwatch or phone timer.
- A chat-abbreviations reference list, for the quickfire round.

## Notes

- Leaders should be aware some Scouts may not have their own phone — pair them with another Scout or use a leader's device for the tasks that need one.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities.
