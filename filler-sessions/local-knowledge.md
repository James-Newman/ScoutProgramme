# Local Knowledge Activity Badge

**Badge:** [Scouts Local Knowledge Activity Badge](https://www.scouts.org.uk/scouts/activity-badges/local-knowledge/) (Option 1: rural and suburban areas)

**Why it's a good filler:** the badge's premise is that Scouts already know their local area from living there — the session's job is to help them demonstrate that knowledge on a map, not to teach it from scratch.

**Adaptation needed:** none. Every task below is taken directly from the badge's own Option 1 wording.

**Requirements completed this session:**

> "Show that you know the local area surrounding your home or Scout Headquarters, up to a radius of 2 kilometres in suburban districts and 5 kilometres in rural districts." Locate as many as you can of: doctors, veterinary surgeons, dentists, hospitals and ambulance station; fire station, police station, garages, shopping centres, retail parks and convenience stores; main bus stops, railway stations and local routes of buses and trains; local Scout Headquarters, public parks, theatres, sports and leisure complexes and cinemas; places of worship, museums, schools, colleges and local government buildings; local routes that take you to the nearest motorway or national routes.
>
> "Use a street map to point out six locations from step 1. From your home or Scout Headquarters, show the quickest route to one of the places. Give clear directions to a place of interest 8 kilometres away, to a person travelling by car or public transport."
>
> "Find out which major local roads link to the motorway and A-road network and the main cities these roads serve."

## Running order

| Time | Duration | Block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | 10 min | Welcome |
| 0:10–0:20 | 10 min | Briefing |
| 0:20–0:50 | 30 min | Locate the amenities |
| 0:50–1:20 | 30 min | Point out six & plan the quickest route |
| 1:20–1:40 | 20 min | Directions to 8km away |
| 1:40–1:50 | 10 min | Major roads & motorway links |
| 1:50–2:00 | 10 min | Close |

## Session detail

### Welcome (10 min)

Notices, opening.

### Briefing (10 min)

Explain the radius (2km suburban, 5km rural — pick whichever applies to the meeting place) and hand out the local map (see Materials).

### Locate the amenities (30 min)

Individually or in pairs, Scouts mark as many as they can find on the map of: medical (doctors, vets, dentists, hospital, ambulance station), emergency/services (fire station, police station, garages), shopping (centres, retail parks, convenience stores), transport (bus stops, railway stations, routes), community (Scout HQ, parks, theatres, sports/leisure centres, cinemas), and civic (places of worship, museums, schools, colleges, local government buildings).

**Materials:** one printed local street map per Scout or pair, covering the correct radius.

### Point out six & plan the quickest route (30 min)

Each Scout picks six of the locations they marked and points them out clearly on the map, then works out and shows the quickest route from home or the meeting place to one of them.

### Directions to 8km away (20 min)

Working from the same map (extended if needed to cover 8km), each Scout writes clear directions to a place of interest roughly 8km away, aimed at someone travelling by car or by public transport — meaning the directions need to reference road names/junctions for a driver and bus/train routes for a public transport user.

### Major roads & motorway links (10 min)

Quick group task: using the map, identify which major local roads connect to the motorway and A-road network, and name the main cities those roads serve. A quickfire question round works well here given the short time.

### Close (10 min)

Notices, thought for the week, home.

## Materials

Printed local street maps or map-based apps/tablets, covering the required radius around the meeting place, at a scale that shows road names clearly. A leader should prepare or print these in advance — this is genuinely local, so no generic version can be provided here.

## Notes

- Since Scouts already live locally, the main variable is map-reading confidence rather than local knowledge — pairing less confident map-readers with more confident ones works well.
- Requirements can be adapted to suit each young person's abilities; a Scout less familiar with the area (a recent joiner) can research rather than recall, which is still legitimately "finding out."
